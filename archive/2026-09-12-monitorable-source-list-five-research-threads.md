Monitorable Source List for a Recurring Claude Scheduled Task: Five Research Threads
TL;DR

* Across all five threads, the highest-value additions are release-schedule-predictable government and university products (Chicago Fed AgLetter quarterly; USDA NASS Land Values/Cash Rents each August; Fed Distributional Financial Accounts quarterly; drought.gov Midwest updates) plus a small set of genuinely analytical AI-economics newsletters (Epoch AI, SemiAnalysis, Ed Zitron) to fill your biggest gap — AI/data-center market risk.
* Most of these have RSS or email subscriptions; the few that don't (Michigan/Indiana forest-health reports, ASFMRA land reports, NCEO's full data, Rutgers working papers) fire only a few times a year and should be scheduled as calendar-triggered fetches, not weekly re-checks.
* A sensible cadence: check ~6 sources weekly (AI/grid/drought), ~10 monthly (Fed ag surveys, farmdoc, Ag Decision Maker, climate reports), and ~12 only on known annual/triennial release dates (USDA Land Values early August, Iowa State survey mid-December, Fed SCF triennial in late 2026, World Inequality Report December).

Key Findings
You already hold strong Purdue-centric ag-economics coverage, environmental monitoring (AirNow/EPA/NWS/MRCC), and Indiana DNR/USFS forestry regulatory sources. The gaps this report fills are: (1) non-Purdue farmland-economics signals, especially the Federal Reserve district surveys and farmland-conversion policy; (2) forest-health reporting (as opposed to regulatory) products for Indiana and Michigan; (3) Midwest dust/soil-erosion and climate narrative products tied to tillage timing; (4) an entirely new AI/data-center/market-risk stack; and (5) recurring wealth-concentration and capital-ownership data releases. The single biggest win is Area 4, where you currently have nothing: Epoch AI, SemiAnalysis, Ed Zitron, the LBNL data-center report, and MISO/interconnection-queue trackers together give both the bull-case capex data and the skeptical/analytical counterweight that matches your Q1-2027 adjustment thesis.

AREA 1 — Farmland Values & Agricultural Land Economics (beyond Purdue)
Top sources for a scheduled task:

1. Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago — AgLetter (Seventh District farmland values & credit survey). URL: https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/agletter/index. Quarterly (published on scheduled days; February, May, August, November issues). Has an email alert list ("Register to receive email alerts when new issues are published"). Free. Unique signal: the most Indiana-relevant quarterly farmland-value series — the Seventh District explicitly covers Indiana, Illinois, Iowa, Wisconsin, and Michigan, and as of 2026 now incorporates Farm Credit System responses. The May 14, 2026 issue (authors David Oppedahl and Elizabeth Kepner, based on 104 District lenders) reported: "Seventh District farmland values in the first quarter of 2026 were 3% higher than a year ago. Values for 'good' agricultural land in the first quarter of 2026 were 1% lower than in the fourth quarter of 2025," and that "annual cash rental rates for district farmland saw a decrease of 3% in 2026 — their second consecutive decrease." Notably, Indiana farmland values fell 2% in Q1 while Indiana cash rents rose 2% — a divergence worth watching.
2. University of Illinois — farmdoc daily. URL: https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/. Publishes an original research-based article every business day; daily email goes to over thirteen thousand subscribers. Free; email subscription plus a citation RSS feed via RePEc (https://ideas.repec.org/s/ags/illufd.html). Unique signal: the deepest running Corn Belt farm-economics analysis — crop budgets, land-value fundamentals, farm policy — and it directly covers Indiana-adjacent conditions. Companion site Farm Policy News (https://farmpolicynews.illinois.edu/) tracks federal farm policy.
3. USDA NASS — Land Values annual summary & Cash Rents. URL: https://quickstats.nass.usda.gov/ (reports via https://www.nass.usda.gov/). U.S. and state estimates released the first week of August every year; county-level cash rents in late August. Free; NASS offers email subscription to reports and a scheduled release calendar. Unique signal: the authoritative state/county farmland-value and cash-rent benchmark. The "Land Values 2024 Summary" (released August 2, 2024) reported U.S. farm real estate "averaged $4,170 per acre for 2024, up $200 per acre (5.0 percent) from 2023," with U.S. cropland at $5,570/acre (+4.7%) and cropland cash rents at a record $160/acre; the 2025 summary later reported $4,350/acre (+4.3%). This is the ground truth your Purdue August survey complements.
4. Iowa State University — Ag Decision Maker & Farmland Value Survey. URL: https://www.extension.iastate.edu/agdm/ (survey at https://www.extension.iastate.edu/agdm/wdvalues.html; interactive CARD Farmland Portal at https://farmland.card.iastate.edu). Ag Decision Maker newsletter is monthly; the ISU Land Value Survey is annual (results published mid-December). Free; monthly newsletter. Unique signal: the longest-running academic farmland survey in the Corn Belt (back to 1941), a useful cross-check on the Chicago Fed and Purdue numbers. The 2024 survey (CARD, Dr. Rabail Chandio, Dec. 17, 2024) "found that farmland values decreased 3.1%, or $369, to $11,467 per acre" as of November 2024, ending a five-year upward trend; the 2025 survey later showed a 0.7% rebound to $11,549/acre.
5. Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City — Ag Credit Survey. URL: https://www.kansascityfed.org/agriculture/ag-credit-survey/. Quarterly; the Center for Agriculture and the Economy also issues a quarterly Agricultural Bulletin and rolling articles. Free; email/subscription available. Unique signal: Tenth District (Plains) farmland and credit conditions — a leading indicator of stress in crop-dependent areas that tends to show up in the eastern Corn Belt later.

Secondary options:

* St. Louis Fed / Ag Finance Monitor — quarterly Eighth District ag credit and land-value survey; free; complements Chicago and KC Fed. FRED (https://fred.stlouisfed.org) also hosts these series with data-alert email subscriptions.
* Indiana farmland-conversion & ownership policy — track solar/data-center land pressure via Indiana-specific outlets. Indiana is 5th nationally for future solar development with the 13,000-acre Mammoth Solar project in northwest Indiana; solar leases run $900–$1,500/acre/year vs. ~$251/acre farm rent. Foreign ownership: as of the 2024 legislative debate (HB 1183), foreign investors owned or leased 401,747 acres of Indiana ag land (~2%). Monitor via Indiana Capital Chronicle and Indiana Farm Bureau. Indiana Solar Status (https://www.indianasolarstatus.com/) is an advocacy tracker — useful for signal but note its explicit pro-agriculture bias.
* American Farmland Owner (https://www.americanfarmlandowner.com/) — "Where Landowners Get Their News," email newsletter; aggregates Fed/USDA releases with a landowner-investor lens. A commercial newsletter, not a primary source.
* ASFMRA / Farmers National Company / Peoples Company / Farmland Partners market reports — these fire irregularly (mostly annual or quarterly market letters) and generally lack RSS; treat as calendar-checked bookmarks. ASFMRA Indiana chapter land-value data is typically embedded in the annual Purdue survey you already have.

AREA 2 — Forest & Tree Health (Indiana & Michigan)
Top sources:

1. Indiana Woodland Steward newsletter. URL: https://www.inwoodlands.org/ (subscribe page linked as "Subscribe to our Digital Newsletter"; also promoted via Purdue FNR). Roughly three issues/year (Volume 35 in 2026, numbers 1–3). Free digital e-newsletter subscription (and hard-copy option). Unique signal: Indiana-specific woodland management, stumpage/timber price reports, invasive-species and prescribed-burn law updates, written by the 11-organization Indiana Woodland Steward Institute (Purdue, DNR, IHLA). The single best Indiana private-woodland complement to your DNR sources.
2. Purdue Extension FNR "Got Nature?" blog. URL: https://www.purdue.edu/fnr/extension/. Rolling posts (several per week). Free; supports email subscription, with a companion FNR YouTube channel (ID That Tree, Woodland Management Moment, Invasive Species playlists). Unique signal: timely how-to and alert content for Central Hardwoods landowners; re-surfaces the Woodland Steward and Hardwood Ecosystem Experiment findings.
3. USFS Forest Inventory & Analysis (FIA) — Indiana products + EVALIDator. URLs: state reports at https://research.fs.usda.gov/programs/fia; EVALIDator at https://apps.fs.usda.gov/Evalidator/evalidator.jsp. "Forests of Indiana" resource update is annual; full summary reports periodic (the 2018 summary was published 2024). Free. Unique signal: the quantitative backbone of Indiana forest condition — species-level volume, growth, mortality. Notably, white ash leads Indiana annual mortality (37.3 million cubic feet) — a direct EAB signal — while yellow-poplar and sugar maple lead volume and growth. The 2026 FIA National User Group Meeting is in Indianapolis (April 14–15, 2026).
4. National Forest Health Highlights series (USFS). URL: https://www.fs.usda.gov/foresthealth/ (state PDFs such as IN_FHH and MI_FHH). Annual, by state. Free; no RSS — calendar-checked. Unique signal: a concise annual per-state synthesis of insect/disease/defoliation conditions (oak decline, EAB, spongy moth, aerial detection survey results) — the reporting complement to your spongy moth regulatory pages.
5. Michigan DNR — Forest Health Highlights (annual report). URL: https://www.michigan.gov/dnr (2025 report on the Forest Health webpage; press releases via MI Newswire). Annual (2025 edition released late April 2026). The DNR distributes forestry news through GovDelivery email bulletins (content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MIDNR). Free. Unique signal: the New Buffalo/Three Oaks-relevant source — tracks hemlock woolly adelgid along the Lake Michigan shoreline, spotted lanternfly spread (now in 8+ SE Michigan counties), oak wilt, and the March 2025 ice-storm salvage across 13 northern counties.

Secondary options:

* USFS Northern Research Station (https://research.fs.usda.gov/nrs) — forest-health publications and webinars; email/alert options; feeds the FIA and Forest Health Highlights products.
* Michigan State University Extension Forestry (https://www.canr.msu.edu/forestry/) — email news digests; also carries Michigan invasive-species content.
* Indiana Forest Alliance (https://www.indianaforestalliance.org/) — advocacy-oriented; email newsletter; useful for state-forest policy/logging signals, but note its conservation-advocacy stance.
* USFS Insect & Disease Detection Survey (IDS) / aerial detection viewers — data viewers updated seasonally; no RSS; calendar-checked. Digital Mobile Sketch Mapping data underlies the annual highlights.
* Purdue Hardwood Ecosystem Experiment (https://www.heeforeststudy.org/) — long-term study; periodic publications, not a high-frequency feed.

AREA 3 — Climate, Dust, Drought & Soil (Midwest-specific)
Top sources:

1. U.S. Drought Monitor + drought.gov Midwest status updates. URLs: map at https://droughtmonitor.unl.edu/; Midwest narrative at https://www.drought.gov/drought-status-updates. The Drought Monitor map updates every Thursday; the Midwest Status Update narrative is issued roughly monthly or as conditions warrant. Free; NIDIS offers email updates. Unique signal: the definitive weekly drought classification plus a Midwest narrative tying in Mississippi River navigation, soil moisture, and streamflow — the March 26, 2026 update noted 23% of the Midwest in D1–D3 with long-term 8–16 inch deficits across Indiana/Illinois. Directly relevant to your dust-storm and tillage-timing interest.
2. farmdoc daily — dust-storm / soil-erosion research thread (University of Illinois). URL: https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/. Daily; free; email + RePEc RSS (same as Area 1). Unique signal: farmdoc published the most rigorous accessible analysis of the 2023 I-55 Illinois dust storm using WRF-Chem modeling of tillage/cover-crop erodibility — precisely the tillage-timing-and-dust mechanism you're researching. Illinois Extension's dust-storm materials (https://extension.illinois.edu/) complement it.
3. USDA Midwest Climate Hub. URL: https://www.climatehubs.usda.gov/hubs/midwest. Rolling posts + periodic assessments; free; email/subscription. Unique signal: USDA-framed adaptation content linking climate to cropping decisions (planting dates, cover crops, soil health) for the exact five-state region.
4. NOAA NCEI — monthly/annual climate reports. URL: https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/monitoring/monthly-report/. National and state climate summaries released monthly (~mid-month for the prior month); annual summary in January. Free; email/RSS available. Unique signal: authoritative monthly state-level temperature/precipitation rankings for Indiana and Michigan — the climate-record backbone.
5. NASA Worldview (MODIS/VIIRS aerosol & true-color imagery). URL: https://worldview.earthdata.nasa.gov/. Near-real-time daily imagery; free; the underlying LANCE feeds update within ~3 hours. No RSS but stable deep-linkable URLs by date/layer. Unique signal: lets you visually confirm dust plumes and aerosol optical depth over the Midwest on any given windy spring day — a direct observational check complementing your NASA Earth Observatory imagery.

Secondary options:

* Indiana State Climate Office at Purdue (https://ag.purdue.edu/indiana-state-climate/; legacy https://iclimate.org/) — publishes the quarterly "Climate INformer" newsletter (launched 2021; subscribe via https://mailimages.purdue.edu/Subscribe/purdue-coa_isco/ClimateINformer.html) plus real-time Purdue Mesonet data and auto-generated climate maps (frost/freeze probabilities, growing degree days). Director: Dr. Beth Hall. Free; email newsletter (no RSS); active on X (@IN_Climate). The single most Indiana-specific climate product — high value for your La Porte and Fountain County land.
* Midwestern Regional Climate Center (mrcc.purdue.edu) — you already have this; note it offers the cli-MATE tool and Midwest Climate Watch with subscribable products.
* USDA NASS Crop Progress reports — weekly during the growing season (Monday afternoons, April–November), free, email/RSS. Unique signal: state-level planting-progress percentages that directly proxy spring tillage timing — the leading indicator for dust-event risk windows.
* Dust Alliance for North America (DANA) — inter-university dust-research partnership (Thomas E. Gill of UT El Paso estimated ~$154.4 billion/year in U.S. wind-erosion/dust losses); publications irregular; no RSS — periodic check.
* USDA NRCS wind-erosion / soil-health resources and annual cover-crop / no-till adoption statistics (via NASS Census of Agriculture and state reports) — low-frequency, calendar-checked.

AREA 4 — AI Industry, Data-Center Buildout & Market Risk (your biggest gap)
Top sources (analytical, not promotional):

1. Epoch AI. URL: https://epoch.ai/ (newsletter at https://epochai.substack.com/). Multiple cadences: Gradient Updates (weekly commentary), Data Insights (weekly), The Epoch Brief (monthly digest), plus sporadic reports and the "Epoch After Hours" podcast. Free email newsletters (Substack, so RSS available). Unique signal: the most rigorous quantitative AI-trends shop. Its Data Insight "Hyperscaler capex has quadrupled since GPT-4's release" (Isabel Juniewicz, Feb. 26, 2026) found that "the combined capital expenditures at Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, and Oracle have been growing at an average of 72% per year since the second quarter of 2023…If this trend continues, they will spend $770 billion in 2026" — noting the five spent a combined $140.6B in Q4 2025 alone. Epoch also tracks data-center power doubling (~every 7–10 months) and maintains an AI companies database (revenue/funding/compute, updated regularly). This is the empirical bull-case data you lack — free and non-hype.
2. SemiAnalysis (Dylan Patel). URL: https://semianalysis.com/ (newsletter https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/). Weekly + ad-hoc; 180,000+ subscribers. Free-tier email newsletter with RSS; a paid Core Research tier and separate institutional data-center/accelerator models (the Datacenter Industry Model tracks 5,000+ facilities, priced for institutions — not needed for monitoring). Unique signal: the deepest semiconductor/data-center supply-chain analysis — power, cooling, chip roadmaps, and the economics beneath the buildout. Free posts alone are high-signal.
3. Ed Zitron — Where's Your Ed At. URL: https://www.wheresyoured.at/ (podcast: Better Offline). Weekly free newsletter (often 10,000–18,000 words) plus a premium tier ($7/mo, $18/quarter, $70/yr). Free tier is substantial; email + RSS. Unique signal: the most detailed skeptical running analysis of AI-company unit economics, circular financing (hyperscalers ↔ AI labs), NVIDIA/OpenAI/Anthropic finances, and data-center fragility — the direct analytical counterweight to Epoch/SemiAnalysis and the best fit for your Q1-2027 adjustment thesis. Explicitly opinionated/bearish — read as a bear-case check, not neutral reporting.
4. Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory — U.S. Data Center Energy Usage Report. URL: https://eta.lbl.gov/publications/2024-lbnl-data-center-energy-usage-report. Periodic (2016, then December 2024; congressionally mandated under the Energy Act of 2020). Free PDF; no RSS — calendar-checked. Unique signal: the authoritative federal estimate. The 2024 report states data centers "consumed about 4.4% of total U.S. electricity in 2023 and…are expected to consume between 6.7 and 12% of total U.S. electricity by 2028," with usage climbing "from 58 TWh in 2014 to 176 TWh in 2023" and estimated to reach "325 to 580 TWh by 2028." A newer LBNL 2025 Update now cites 192 TWh (4.7%) for 2024 and a 464 TWh 2028 reference case — check the ETA/energyanalysis.lbl.gov pages for the latest edition. The anchor number for every grid-demand debate.
5. MISO large-load & interconnection-queue data + interconnection.fyi. URLs: https://www.misoenergy.org/planning/large-loads---container-page/large-load-additions/ and the independent tracker https://www.interconnection.fyi/ (Indiana data-center page: https://www.interconnection.fyi/data-center/state/IN). MISO posts planning updates periodically; interconnection.fyi updates daily and offers email alerts. Free. Unique signal: the Indiana/MISO-specific grid view you wanted — most of Indiana is in MISO (northeast Indiana in PJM). MISO's 2024 long-term load forecast attributes 149–241 TWh of 2024–2044 growth to data centers; Indiana Michigan Power (AEP) expects peak demand to roughly double to 8 GW by 2030 on hyperscaler growth. interconnection.fyi's daily-updated queue and data-center trackers are the closest thing to a live feed.

Secondary options:

* Data Center Dynamics (https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/) — daily industry news, free, email + RSS; more news than analysis but the best breadth on facility-level announcements (siting, power deals, outages). Good for Indiana siting alerts.
* Stratechery (Ben Thompson) (https://stratechery.com/) — free "Weekly Article" + paid daily Updates ($15/mo or $150/yr); email + RSS. Strategic/business-model analysis of the hyperscalers; more strategy than capex data.
* The Information (https://www.theinformation.com/) — subscription (~$400/yr); email; strong original reporting on AI-lab finances and data-center deals; paywalled, so lower priority unless you want scoops.
* EIA Electricity data (https://www.eia.gov/electricity/) — monthly Electric Power Monthly and Short-Term Energy Outlook; free; email/RSS. Authoritative U.S. electricity demand/supply context.
* IEEFA (Institute for Energy Economics and Financial Analysis) and RMI — periodic skeptical/analytical reports on AI-driven overbuild risk; free; email. IEEFA's Kunkel & Wamsted argued (June 2025) the "risk of AI-driven, overbuilt infrastructure is real."
* Where to be skeptical: vendor blogs, hyperscaler press releases, and "AI product news" aggregators are promotional — exclude. Epoch (data), SemiAnalysis (supply chain), Zitron (bear case), and LBNL/EIA (official) form a balanced, low-hype core.

AREA 5 — Inequality, Capital Formation & Asset Ownership
Top sources for the "mechanism of compounding" question:

1. Federal Reserve — Distributional Financial Accounts (DFA). URL: https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/efa/efa-distributional-financial-accounts.htm (announcements feed at .../dfa/announcements/; also on FRED). Quarterly, updated ~10–11 weeks after each quarter's end. Free; the announcements page functions as a change feed and FRED offers email data alerts. Unique signal: the timeliest quarterly measure of U.S. wealth shares by percentile (top 1%, next 9%, next 40%, bottom 50%) — the closest thing to a live tracker of wealth concentration, and it decomposes by asset type (business equity, real estate, etc.), letting you watch the mechanism of compounding, not just a static Gini.
2. Federal Reserve — Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF). URL: https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/scfindex.htm. Triennial (2019, 2022, 2025); the 2025 survey began fielding March 2025, so results are expected in late 2026. Free; the SCF index page is the calendar-checked anchor. Unique signal: the only fully representative U.S. household balance-sheet microdata — homeownership and asset holdings by wealth decile, the foundation the DFA quarterly series is built on. This is the definitive "who owns what" release; put its next publication on the calendar.
3. World Inequality Database / World Inequality Lab. URLs: https://wid.world/ and https://inequalitylab.world/. The WID database updates annually; the flagship World Inequality Report is periodic (2018, 2022, and the World Inequality Report 2026 released December 10, 2025, lead author Ricardo Gómez-Carrera with Thomas Piketty and Lucas Chancel). Free; email newsletter. Unique signal: the Piketty/Saez/Zucman-network long-run income and wealth series you asked for — global and long-horizon. The WIR 2026 Executive Summary reports that "the wealthiest 0.001% alone, fewer than 60,000 multi-millionaires, control today three times more wealth than half of humanity combined. Their share has grown steadily from almost 4% in 1995 to over 6% today," while the top 10% own 75% of global wealth versus 2% for the bottom half.
4. Ownership Works. URL: https://ownershipworks.org/our-impact/. Annual Impact Report (2023, 2024, 2025). Free; email newsletter (no RSS). Unique signal: the investor-side, real-dollar measure of broad-based ownership actually reaching workers. The 2025 Impact Report states "$1.7 billion has already been paid out to tens of thousands of workers outside of the C-suite via liquidity events to date," with "$12.4 billion in additional projected wealth to come," across "more than 268,000 employees" — from 41 liquidity events, with 20 exits averaging $55,000 per employee-owner and 74% of employee-owners low/moderate-income. This is the "wealth compounding into workers' hands" signal from the private-equity deal side — a concrete mechanism source.
5. National Center for Employee Ownership (NCEO). URL: https://www.nceo.org/research. The Employee Ownership Report newsletter is bimonthly (~6 issues/year); survey/research reports annual (e.g., 2026 ESOP Recruitment & Retention Survey; 2025 Corporate Governance Survey). Partially paywalled — the newsletter, full survey reports, and Q&A database require paid membership; some statistics and the free microsites ownershipeconomy.org and esopinfo.org are open. Unique signal: the authoritative running count of the U.S. ESOP universe (company counts, participant numbers, benchmarking) — the scale-and-prevalence measure of the ownership mechanism.

Secondary options:

* Rutgers Institute for the Study of Employee Ownership and Profit Sharing (Beyster). URL: https://smlr.rutgers.edu/faculty-research-engagement/institute-study-employee-ownership-and-profit-sharing (working papers at .../working-paper-series; curriculum library at https://cleo.rutgers.edu). Working papers released ad hoc; no self-hosted RSS or email newsletter — must be checked periodically. Free. Unique signal: the peer-vetted academic evidence on whether employee ownership causally raises wages and wealth — the "does the mechanism work" layer.
* Washington Center for Equitable Growth. URL: https://equitablegrowth.org/. Rolling near-daily research posts; free; email newsletter (RSS likely at /feed/ but unconfirmed). Unique signal: rigorous U.S. data on how top-end wealth concentration constrains broad growth, plus antitrust/merger-threshold and distributional-measurement work — several of the specific policy levers you listed.
* Aspen Institute Financial Security Program. URL: https://www.aspeninstitute.org/programs/financial-security-program/. Research reports irregular; free; email newsletters (Benefits21 list + central Aspen portal at https://www.aspeninstitute.org/newsletters/). Unique signal: the household-balance-sheet and "job lock"/employer-decoupled-benefits research angle — directly matches your interest in health-insurance job lock and asset-building policy. Note: "The Bottom Line" is a one-off 2017 report, not a recurring newsletter — do not schedule it; monitor the FSP insights library and Benefits21 email list instead.
* Congressional Budget Office — Trends in the Distribution of Family Wealth. Periodic (last major edition covered 1989–2022); free; CBO offers email alerts. Good triangulation against the SCF/DFA.
* Our World in Data — you have this; note its inequality/wealth pages update when source data update and it offers change notifications.

Benchmarks that would change this plan:

* If your AI-adjustment thesis is the priority, weight Stage 1 toward Epoch/SemiAnalysis/Zitron and add The Information (paid) once a specific catalyst window (e.g., a hyperscaler capex-guidance quarter) approaches.
* If a Midwest drought re-intensifies (Drought Monitor crosses into D2–D3 across Indiana in early spring), promote NASA Worldview and Crop Progress to twice-weekly during the March–May tillage window.
* If Indiana passes new farmland-conversion or foreign-ownership legislation, add the Indiana Capital Chronicle and IURC docket to Stage 2.

Caveats

* Forward-looking vs. realized data: Several AI/grid figures are projections, not realized outcomes — LBNL's 6.7–12% by 2028, MISO's 2024–2044 load-growth ranges, and Epoch's "$770 billion in 2026" capex figure are scenario extrapolations ("if this trend continues") and should be read as such. Bank/lender expectation questions in the Fed ag surveys are also sentiment, not transactions.
* Bias flags: Ed Zitron is explicitly bearish/opinionated; SemiAnalysis and Epoch skew toward documenting rapid growth; Indiana Solar Status and Indiana Forest Alliance are advocacy sources; American Energy Alliance (cited on solar/farmland) is an advocacy group. Use these for signal and triangulate against primary data.
* Subscription/paywall status can change: SemiAnalysis, Zitron, and Stratechery all have substantial free tiers today but gate their deepest work; The Information and NCEO's full data are paywalled. Verify at signup.
* RSS availability is uneven: Government pages (NASS, NCEI, Fed, drought.gov) and Substack newsletters (Epoch, Zitron) have reliable feeds; state forest-health reports, LBNL, ASFMRA, Ownership Works, and Rutgers do not — those must be calendar-checked, which is exactly why the staged cadence above separates "feed-driven" from "date-driven" sources.
* Coverage gaps to confirm at setup: exact 2026 NASS release dates; whether the Midwest Climate Hub, Indiana Forest Alliance, and Equitable Growth offer true RSS (vs. email only); and whether a successor LBNL data-center report beyond the 2025 Update is scheduled.
