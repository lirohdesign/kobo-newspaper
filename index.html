.DISCUSSION... Issued at 257 AM EDT Fri Oct 16 2026

As a mid level trough digs across the Central US, an upper low gets shoved northeastward pushing a surface low into southeast Canada/Northeast US. At the same time, a 1040 mb surface high slides southeastward into the Central CONUS. The presence of this strong surface high often indicates the arrival of a cooler airmass and that is the case this time. Given our departure of above normal temperatures and the arrival of this cool airmass, we`ll have some hazards to contend with today and this weekend. First, yesterday`s cold front shifts eastward and the vigorous vort max associated with the mid level trough dives through Lk MI late this afternoon into this evening. Given the warm lake and the 850 mb temps dipping to around or below 0C, lake enhancement is expected to cause rain. A surface trough is expected to swing through the southeast area of Lk MI around 18z with ample low level moisture, low level wind convergence, and building lake enhanced instability. Given the movement of this trough and swiveling trajectories from northwest to north, the area with the best chance to see "higher" QPF is southern Berrien into LaPorte where 0.25 to 0.5" is forecast, tapering off the farther away from the lake you are. The heaviest of the rain appears to get into Berrien county midday (15 or 16z) and continues swiveling southward through the afternoon and into the evening. The other hazard is wind gusts, which look to get underway along lk MI- adjacent locations around 19 or 20z before pulling away around 3z. Given the surface trough, there could be some isentropic downglide enough to work an isolated 45 kt gust down to the surface. Otherwise, it appears that frequent gusts between 30 and 40 mph will be possible during that time as low level lapse rates become conducive. The strongest of the gusts are adjacent to the lake, tapering off the farther away you get. This is why the wind advisory was hoisted for Lk MI- adjacent locales.

The mention of the cold air also brings to mind the chance for frost. Especially with overnight lows dropping into the 30s from from tonight/Sat AM to Sunday night/Mon AM and potentially again Tue AM. The main issue with Friday night/Sat AM is the gradient which could keep winds strong enough to eliminate formation of frost. A lesser possibility is lake clouds hang on, but would probably lean towards subsidence on the eastern side of the lake clouds removing them. Clouds appear to already be dissipating on the NAM by around 6 to 9z for maybe all of our counties (lowest confidence on LaPorte to White county line). This would give areas along or about IN-15 north of US-24 on into MI the best chance to see frost tonight. Surface high pressure continues to poke into the area Saturday morning with the stronger gradient lurking to our north. So, cloud cover appears less of factor. It`s often that as return flow begins to occur that we get our best frost chances as winds slacken, but the boundary layer is able to get back to what it was the previous night or potentially colder. So, Saturday night has the best chance for frost. There is also the possibility of a freeze primarily along and south of US-24 Sun AM with a couple hours of temps right around there then.

Surface high pressure then becomes stagnant across the eastern CONUS as blocking builds across the Atlantic. The NAO/AO both drop negative during this time. The upper jet also retreats back up into Canada. A slow moving trough moves into the western CONUS, but takes its time arriving into the area potentially continuing to provide dry weather into next weekend. Temperatures trend warmer with highs returning to the 70s during the next work week and lows warming slowly through the 40s.

02. nyt briefing

By Sam Sifton

Good morning. Let’s look at what happens when school police arrest children.

There are a lot of police officers in Texas public schools. In the four years since a gunman killed 21 people at an elementary school in Uvalde, districts across the state have spent billions of dollars to put cops on every campus. They’re there both to protect against future attacks and to curb misbehavior and fighting, which swelled after the Covid pandemic.

The crackdown has had an unintended consequence, according to an investigation by The Times and The San Antonio Express-News: It has swept tens of thousands of elementary students and middle schoolers into the justice system. In the 2024-25 school year, the most recent one for which data is available, about 4,800 Texans between the ages of 10 and 13 were referred for prosecution. That’s a third more than the number a decade before.

What “referred for prosecution” means in plain English: Some kid slaps another kid on the playground, or puffs on a vape in the bathroom, or kicks a principal in frustration or rage, and a uniformed officer with a gun puts the child in handcuffs. Just like that the student’s not just in the school system, but in the criminal justice one, too. That can reshape a life.

Some referred children receive a warning. Others end up before a judge, where they’re assigned community service, counseling or court supervision. In more serious cases, they end up in state juvenile justice facilities.

In few cases do they return to school unchanged. Research shows that getting handcuffed and dragged out of school can hurt academic performance and can lead to future contact with law enforcement. Referrals are one of the places where the so-called school-to-prison pipeline starts.

‘She just gave up’

The stories our reporters recount are chilling. In 2024, a 12-year-old girl in Galveston got sent to the principal’s office for being “disruptive.” School officials suspended her and told her to go wait in the lobby for her mother to pick her up.

She refused to go there. The administrators called for a school police officer, who arrived and told the girl: “You’ve got four seconds to walk over there. Otherwise, I’m going to put you in handcuffs, and we’re going to give you a ride.”

The girl didn’t move. “OK, I’m done,” the officer said, and slid off her pink backpack to handcuff her wrists behind her back.

His body camera footage shows the sequence:

“I didn’t really know why it was happening,” the girl told our reporters later. “Why would they put me in handcuffs?”

The girl was eventually charged with trespassing. Two former school district police chiefs who reviewed the case at The Times’s request said that the arrest and the referral were unnecessary. But the girl pleaded guilty and was put on probation for a year, her mother told our reporters. And now her daughter has even more trouble in class.

“She just gave up,” her mother said.

The witness

Kristian Hernández is one of the reporters who worked on the investigation. A native Texan, he grew up on the border and attended public schools in El Paso. Kristian was a straight-A student, but one of those straight-A students with long heavy-metal hair and a skateboard, the kind who gets in fights. He had plenty of contact with law enforcement along the way. The arrests he documented in schools didn’t surprise him, he told me yesterday. It was the age of those arrested.

“There’s a lot of emphasis and even support for making schools more secure,” he said. “I haven’t talked to a single kid or parent who hasn’t expressed a fear of a school shooting.” That fear is understandable. Just yesterday, three kids and a teacher were killed at a school in Oregon.

Still, Kristian continued: “What we learned that was surprising — and scary — is that no one is paying attention to what happens when schools are hardened against that threat.”

Among other things, it’s 10-year-olds getting placed in the back of police cars, and 12-year-olds asking for lawyers.

Please read the whole story, which is by Kristian, Asher Lehrer-Small, Clare Amari and Rosie Cima.
