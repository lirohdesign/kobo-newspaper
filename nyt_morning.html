A.I. by the numbers

Which artificial intelligence firm will become the biggest company ever to debut on the stock market? Anthropic offered the opening bid on Monday, when it released its plan to offer shares on the Nasdaq exchange.

The numbers are staggering. In the first half of this year, Anthropic earned $16.3 billion. In that same period, it lost $80.7 billion. But it’s growing fast. Its potential valuation in the initial public offering is $2 trillion.

Here’s what that number looks like, written out: $2,000,000,000,000. It’s hard to imagine. Two thousand billion dollars. The average U.S. household’s annual income is $126,700, according to the Census Bureau. That household would need nearly 16 million years in order to reach $2 trillion, and then only if it saved every single penny.

As companies and investors wrestle with the colossal size of the A.I. economy — and as the markets they serve grow anxious about whether it’s all just a bubble, sure to burst — Evan Gorelick and I thought an index would be a useful way to examine what’s happening. Numbers (even ginormous ones!) can help us understand what’s happening in the world.

A data center in Austin, Texas, in 2024. Christie Hemm Klok for The New York Times

ASK THE MORNING

How do other countries draw their legislative maps? | Mark Price | Commerce Township, Michigan

Many democracies take political mapmaking away from the politicians. Britain and Canada use independent, nonpartisan commissions. Australia’s commission includes a judge and a statistician, and it gets comments from the public before setting final boundaries. In France, the government drafts the changes, but an independent commission, a constitutional council and Parliament all review them. Hungary under Viktor Orban was one exception. His party redrew districts to strengthen his supermajority. But that goes only so far: Orban was decisively voted out of office earlier this year. — Christina Morales, a reporter on our National desk
