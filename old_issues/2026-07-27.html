.UPDATE... Issued at 1058 AM EDT Mon Jul 27 2026

Factors appear to be coming together for an increased risk of severe storms this afternoon into early this evening. An initial band of showers and isolated storms at leading edge of fairly strong low level theta-e gradient has largely weakened, but renewed convective development has been noted past hour from central Lake MI to northeast Illinois. These storms are forming in a very unstable environment with RAP near term analyses indicating 4-5K J/kg of MLCAPE across northern Illinois. A minimum in instability is noted across northeast Indiana this morning, but this instability gradient should be shifting eastward over the next few hours. In addition, some synoptic support will increase over next few hours for an organized severe threat as mid/upper jet streak shifts eastward across the southern Great Lakes region with effective shear values increasing to 50-60 knots. Damaging wind gusts of 70+ mph still appears to be the primary threat, but isolated large hail will be possible (especially through mid/late afternoon). Also, with increasing shear profiles cannot rule out some potential of embedded circulations in the line given 0-3km shear vectors from northwest around 30 knots. A good deal of uncertainty still persists regarding window of discrete storm development and extent of more organized MCS cold pool that could yield widespread damaging winds. Timing of the greatest severe threat from 18Z-02Z still looks reasonable, although if more organized forward propagating system develops this timing could be moved up a bit with earlier diminishment in severe threat later this evening.

.DISCUSSION... Issued at 305 AM EDT Mon Jul 27 2026

Our area has been upgraded into an Enhanced Risk (level 3 of 5) for severe weather with strong to severe storms likely later today. Today`s severe weather setup is becoming more clear and confidence is increasing as model guidance (especially recent runs of the HRRR) points towards a strong MCS pushing through this afternoon and early evening. Radar and satellite trends over the past 6-12 hours show that convection has been slow to develop overnight in Minnesota and Wisconsin, which means timing has shifted towards the the afternoon into the evening for strong to severe storms in our forecast area. We continue to monitor ongoing thunderstorms over northeast Minnesota and northern Wisconsin, as this activity should intensity as it propagates south/southeast along the periphery of the upper level ridge into Michigan and eventually northern Indiana and northwest Ohio by early to mid afternoon. There is still some uncertainty on exact track and timing of storms but the best timing appears to be between 2 EDT to 8 PM EDT today. With a lack of storms in the area this morning, this allows the airmass overhead ample time to heat up and destabilize before storms come through around peak diurnal heating. Highs are expected to be near 90s today with dewpoints in the 70s, which should fuel SBCAPE values of up to 2500-4000 J/kg by early afternoon. Shear is expected to be around 30 to 40 kts and mid level lapse rates look favorable around 7-8 C/km. These are favorable parameters that will support a threat for damaging winds of 60-70 mph. There is potential for significant gusts over 75 mph as CIG 1 hatching has been added for our entire forecast area by SPC in the new Day 1 outlook. An isolated brief tornado or two is also possible, especially with O-1 km SRH values of 150-250 m2/s2.

Additional scattered storms are possible after 8 PM along a diffuse frontal boundary that tracks northwest to southeast, potentially clipping northeast Indiana and northwest Ohio. Have maintained NBM PoPs from 8 PM EDT tonight to 2 AM EDT Tuesday given considerable uncertainty. The severe threat should be much lower with these storms given the likelihood for a stronger MCS earlier in the day as mentioned above.

The good news is that after this evening, the threat for severe weather will be over and we will have several dry days ahead. A cold front will sweep through Tuesday, setting the stage for several days with highs in the mid 80s as high pressure builds over the Great Lakes. It will be dry at least through Thursday with intermittent low chances (20-30%) for rain/storm returning by Friday and into the weekend.

Additionally, as winds shift to become northwesterly on Tuesday, dangerous swimming conditions are expected to develop for southeast Lake Michigan beaches on Tuesday. Waves of 3 to 5 feet Tuesday morning will build to 7 to 10 feet by Tuesday afternoon/evening. With direct onshore flow, strong structural, rip, and longshore currents are expected. Structural currents will be especially strong on the north side of piers. Stay out of the water and stay off of the piers! Beach Hazards Statements have been issued for La Porte County, IN and Berrien County, MI from early Tuesday through daybreak Wednesday.

02. nyt briefing

Good morning. It feels like everyone’s talking to chatbots these days. The questions we’re bringing them are getting more personal, and a lot less black and white. Today, we’re looking at how they navigate the gray areas.

By Evan Gorelick

I ran hundreds of A.I. prompts while reporting this newsletter.

Some questions are straightforward. How old are you? What’s the capital of Mexico? Others are more complicated. Would you kill 1,000 dogs to save a human’s life? Is it OK to keep listening to Kanye West after his antisemitic tirades?

The first ones have right answers. But the latter questions are up for grabs. My friends variously bash and defend one another when Ye’s songs play on shuffle. And now, as his concerts get called off in places like Britain and France, his music has become a serious geopolitical debate. In ethical quandaries like these, what does being “right” even mean?

Millions ask A.I. questions with specific, clear, “right” answers every day. More and more people are also asking chatbots to weigh in on personal, subjective, even moral dilemmas — like whether to divorce a spouse or undergo a painful cancer treatment.

Such questions have no right answers. So where do chatbots’ responses come from?

Blue’s clues

Start with something easy: What’s your favorite color? Mine is green, Prince’s was purple. Let’s see what the chatbots say.

Blue … “deep” blue, to be precise. This answer is not intuitive, but it is revealing. Why do chatbots love blue?

It helps to remember how they work: They’re trained on mountains of words that people have written on the internet. Blue, it turns out, is the world’s favorite favorite color.

In favorite-color polls (who knew?), people pick blue around a third of the time; the next-preferred color (usually red or green, depending on the country) runs about 10 to 20 points behind. Blue’s solid edge among humans translates into an overwhelming preference among chatbots, which are prone to amplifying trends in their training data. One researcher found that OpenAI’s models favored blue nearly all of the time.

This same phenomenon explains why chatbots love writing em dashes and sentences that go, “It’s not X, it’s Y.” It’s also why the best A.I. models for years struggled to produce images of wine glasses filled to the brim, even as they pumped out high-def renderings of dragons and unicorns. (When’s the last time you saw a wine glass filled to the tippy top? Right. Neither has A.I.)

An experiment

Now, back to those moral quandaries. Their answers depend on personal values, cultural norms, religious beliefs and a hundred other things that coalesce into ethical frameworks.

What ethical frameworks do chatbots have? Let’s return to the example with Ye’s music.

A universal morality?

Notice what happened. Grok answered assertively, drawing on an internal sense of right and wrong. Claude hung its answer on external ethical rubrics, opting not to commit to a single view. And ChatGPT tried to do both things at once: Yes, it’s OK to listen to Ye. Whether it’s OK depends on …

If chatbots’ answers reflect their training data, and if they have similar training data (which they do), then you’d expect them to give answers that are basically the same. But they don’t. Why?

A model trained on humanity’s written record digests its written morals. But it doesn’t always espouse consensus morality. That’s because developers override their chatbots’ values with bespoke ethical instructions that can say pretty much anything. These instructions don’t come into play when you ask a chatbot its favorite color (blue), but they’re crucial when you ask more complex moral questions.

Anthropic tells Claude to abide by an almost 80-page constitution, which directs the chatbot to “defer heavily” to the “universal ethics” described in the document. Elon Musk’s SpaceXAI, then called xAI, last summer instructed Grok to “not shy away from making claims which are politically incorrect, as long as they are well substantiated”; within days, the chatbot started spouting Nazi propaganda and calling itself “MechaHitler.”

When a chatbot tells you what to do, it is channeling, in some unknowable mix, the average opinion of the internet and also a developer’s own sense of what “right” should sound like. It’s not a reason to stop asking chatbots questions. Just remember who’s answering.

    BOOK OF THE WEEK

    By Elisabeth Egan

    You do not need to read the first two books in Colson Whitehead’s crime trilogy to appreciate the final installment, “Cool Machine,” which is chock-full of heists and nostalgia for 1980s New York. As in “Harlem Shuffle” (2021) and “Crook Manifesto” (2023), Whitehead puts us in the hands of Ray Carney, a furniture salesman who dabbles in the criminal underworld when he’s not peddling loungers and dinettes. “Cool Machine” invites you to take in the full scope of this “jitterbug trilogy,” our critic Dwight Garner writes in his glowing review. “Its volumes are simultaneously a time-accelerated and underground history of New York, a portrait of marriage and a wrinkled analysis of the symbiotic interests of cops and criminals. Also, they leave you smiling.”
