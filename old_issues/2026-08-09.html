.DISCUSSION... Issued at 301 AM EDT Sun Aug 9 2026

Plenty of uncertainty in the forecast today, yet, depending on which solution verifies, heavy rainfall may prove the most likely hazard this evening along the Indiana-Michigan state line. This is in addition to the damaging wind gust and tornado risk from severe thunderstorms this afternoon (low confidence) and overnight (medium confidence).

As it goes here in northern Indiana, rounds of storms are highly dependent on how any convection earlier in the day evolves. Early this morning, the area to watch is over the ND-SD state line where a growing complex of storms exists. This complex drifts east and into northeast IL and into IN this afternoon. Preexisting high pressure may be the element guidance is struggling with, especially in contrast to the incoming warm front which is timed well to support afternoon convection. Mid-level lapse rates of about 6.8 C/km could be better. Aloft, a 45-knot jet could be enough to sustain storms, yet the best upper-level support is well north across the upper Great Lakes. Ensemble high resolution guidance generally suggests a weakening line of convection arrives by mid-afternoon and presents a non-zero chance for damaging wind gusts amid 1500 j/kg of CAPE and about 30 knots of shear.

Given the anticipated modest state of this afternoon`s convection, round two overnight could prove more potent. Starting with the heavy rain concern, multiple forecast models are latching onto a complex of rain and storms that becomes anchored over south- central Lake Michigan extending east through the Indiana-Michigan state line. Storms develop along the warm front amid a strengthening low-level jet. Michiana communities from La Porte and Michigan city, east to the greater Elkhart-Goshen metro areas could be at risk for urban flooding from rain rates of at least 1" per hour (likely more) and rainfall totals in excess of 2-2.5 inches if forecasts verify. Boundary-parallel flow (which is especially weak and convergent at the surface), 500-mb dew point depressions of 1 to 2 C indicate a very deep moisture profile, along with warm cloud depths nearing 14,000 ft. Swift 850-mb flow of about 30-40 knots and generally dry recent conditions may work to reduce the flood risk. Nonetheless, if training storms can develop, heavy rain and localized urban flooding are a concern tonight.

In addition to the flooding threat, any supercells that develop in the vicinity of the warm front pose a risk for all hazards including tornadoes. Lapse Rates once again pose a challenge, as does overall storm evolution, but 0-1km SRH of nearly 300 m2/s2 results in shallow but looping hodographs. The previously mentioned moisture-rich atmosphere may prove challenging for hail, but as storms congeal into a line, damaging wind gusts would become the primary hazard through early Monday morning.

We`ll face another possible round of severe thunderstorms late Monday afternoon and evening as energetic upper-level flow remains in place. This too could pose a heavy rain risk with many of the same ingredients in place. Tuesday will be worth watching for more storms until high pressure finally noes in by Wednesday to steer this active pattern elsewhere. Late-week low pressure brings another chance for storms. High temperatures will be near to slightly below normal through the 7-day period.

We have more news below. But first, a dispatch from Major League Baseball’s most blessed team.

By Tania Ganguli

In 2024, the White Sox set a record for the most losses in modern Major League Baseball history, with 121. More than a month into the 2025 season, things weren’t looking much better. The Sox were firmly in control of last place in the A.L. Central. Their average home attendance was the fourth worst in baseball.

Then, a miracle happened. Or something like it.

The papal conclave at the Vatican selected Robert Prevost to be the new pope. He was an American, a first for the Roman Catholic Church. And Pope Leo XIV, as he’s now known, was also a White Sox fan.

Since then, the White Sox have made a dramatic turnaround. They are now in first place in their division, and while there are real, baseball-related reasons the team is better (they added some talented players during their down years and are better managed), it’s more fun to wonder if this is all because their most famous fan is the Holy Father.

After all, some funny things have happened around the ballpark since then.

During a game against the Kansas City Royals on June 26, pitcher Mike Vasil wore a microphone for the TV broadcast. Catcher Drew Romo, who is Catholic, approached him in the dugout.

“Pope Leo, if you’re watching this game right now, say a quick prayer for a Jacob Gonzalez homer right now,” Romo said so the mic could catch it.

Seconds later, Gonzalez hit his first home run at Rate Field. Vasil bellowed, “It’s working, Leo!”

I went to Chicago last month to get a sense of the excitement and speak to people in the organization. I attended the no-frills Mass that the team hosts before every Sunday home game, where stadium workers sit next to players, coaches and executives at gray picnic tables. I visited the seat where the pope sat for Game 1 of the 2005 World Series (an incredible view, especially for a World Series game) and, for likely the only time in my life, saw the world as the pope might have seen it.

Mike Downey, the team’s senior director of promotions and marketing, tried so hard to make a pope bobblehead happen. He was planning his daughter’s baptism when I reached out Thursday night with some fact-checking. The bobblehead idea, he told me, was the third thought he had when he found out the pope was a White Sox fan — after “Oh my god,” and “Can we get him to throw out the first pitch one day?”

The church was less excited by the bobblehead idea, though I do wonder if the pope secretly finds it fun; most of the American Catholics I spoke with did. Alas, a Vatican press official did not respond to my email requesting an interview.

Downey ultimately went with a pope hat promotion: At this Tuesday’s game, about 40,000 people will get White Sox-themed papal miters as a gift with their tickets.

I had wondered if some of this pope-mania might border on offensive, but the White Sox were very careful to be respectful. They consulted clergy members as they made their plans. And there’s a strong Catholic identity that permeates the organization.

For Brooks Boyer, an observant Catholic who is the team’s chief marketing officer, having any kind of association with the pope was heady stuff.

“We’ve had sitting mayors of Chicago, we’ve had governors of Illinois that have been avid White Sox fans, we had a president of the United States in Barack Obama,” he said. “I didn’t expect anything bigger than the mayor when I started this job, but we’ve kind of climbed the ladder. What’s next? I mean, I feel like we’re one step away from God.”
