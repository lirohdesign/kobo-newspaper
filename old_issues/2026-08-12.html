Another challenging and very low confidence convection forecast for today. Latest surface obs and mesoanalysis show frontal boundary draped right along our southern CWA border. North of this front some patchy fog has developed but visibilities are highly variable and guidance suggests that will continue through the morning as cloud cover increases so no plans for a dense fog advisory at this time. This front will slowly lift NE through the early morning as the nocturnal LLJ ramps up. Already seeing some convection fire along this front but luckily it remains elevated with virtually no surface- based instability in our area. Will still have to keep an eye on this activity through the early morning though with some low-end potential for hail and heavy rain in a region already water-logged. Hi-res CAM`s are struggling immensely to capture this activity and subsequently predict evolution through the early morning. Forecast confidence is low but based on overall mesoscale setup expect this activity to continue and slowly expand NE through the early morning as the LLJ intensifies and pushes very high theta-e air back into our CWA (850mb dewpoints in excess of 16C).

There could be a brief lull during the late morning but attention this turns to the potential for another MCS associated with convection currently over SD. Here again confidence is not high but hi-res guidance shows a little better agreement in an MCS tracking through at least our southern half around midday. Unfortunately all of the ingredients will be similar to yesterday (very high instability and deep-layer shear) and a damaging squall line is a possibility. Slight southward displacement of frontal boundary may support a focus more toward central IN as reflected in the latest SWODY1. However, it should be noted that several hi-res members track this MCS further north with a track, timing, and intensity eerily similar to yesterday. Straight-line winds would again be the main concern but hail and an embedded tornado can`t be ruled out.

The other issue will be heavy rain. Placement of the front and convection predicted by several guidance members unfortunately suggest the same area hit hard with heavy rain yesterday could be in line to get another round of heavy rain today. Earlier noted convective uncertainties remain but felt confidence enough to extend the flood watch for our southern zones that received significant rain yesterday. Ran the flood watch until 03Z to match up with neighbors but if the midday convection plays out as expected it could be canceled early.

With plethora of near-term challenges and uncertainties, did not spend a lot of time on the later periods. Unfortunately, this front hangs around through at least Fri morning with potential for several more rounds of convection. The front finally does briefly surge north late Fri into Sat which would bring some temporary relief before the associated cold front arrives Sunday. Behind this front, a more lasting period of dry weather is anticipated for early next week.

    Sometimes a little trickery helps keep presidents safe.

    In 2003, officials said that George W. Bush would be spending Thanksgiving at his Texas ranch, but he absconded overnight to Baghdad to surprise American troops there. Bush’s wife and parents were in the dark — and so were forces loyal to Saddam Hussein that might have attacked Air Force One. Three years before that, Bill Clinton quietly slipped into an unmarked plane on his way to defuse nuclear tensions between Pakistan and India. The regular Air Force One, with Secret Service agents aboard, flew the same route as a decoy. “Let your plans be dark and impenetrable as night,” as Sun Tzu put it.

    That appears to be what President Trump had in mind after last month’s NATO summit. He told reporters he would leave Turkey on an older version of Air Force One (rather than the Qatari-donated 747 that he had flown in on). He went so far as to walk on board the jet and wave to news cameras. But it was all a ruse: There was a credible threat against the president from Iran, officials said. So Trump climbed into a catering truck and rode to a military jet nearby.

    How the deception unfolded

    Trump boarded the powder-blue Air Force One from the plane’s left side. The catering container — a truck with a giant metal box on rising stilts — was parked on the right. Footage shows the truck backing away as journalists board the back of the jet. Once on board, reporters were told to keep their window shades down, an unusual request.

    Trump then flew on the military jet from Turkey to Britain. There, the president was secretly brought back to the older Air Force One by vehicle, without reporters noticing. Soon after, he walked off the plane, pretending that he had been on board all along.

    The scheme was developed and carried out by a small group of top officials, including Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, after U.S. intelligence learned about a specific threat of a surface-to-air missile against whatever plane Trump would be flying on. (I was surprised to learn yesterday that Clinton, too, had once ridden off Air Force One in a catering container during a trip to Finland.)

    One thing made this presidential deception different from previous ones, Peter Baker writes. Reporters believed they were flying alongside the president. In truth, officials had turned a plane full of them and other government employees into unknowing bait for an Iranian strike. Peter talked to one former press secretary who compared them to “human shields.”

    For more: Listen to today’s episode of “The Daily,” where the White House correspondent Maggie Haberman talks about the operation’s risks.

    THE PATRIOT GAMES

    Here’s a dispatch from my colleague Zolan Kanno-Youngs, a White House reporter, who was in Ohio yesterday to cover a new Trump-backed competition called the Patriot Games.

    It was Ninja Warrior meets high school sports meets MAGA.

    The event, organized as part of the country’s 250th birthday celebration, offered students — a girl and a boy from each state — a chance to win a combined $250,000 toward college scholarships. The president watched as teens raced through a neon-lit obstacle course that included monkey bars, climbing nets and swinging rings. A fog machine shrouded the proceedings in mist.

    At times, the competition had the feeling of a Trump rally. One attendee shouted “2028” after the president arrived, prompting Trump to smile. “U.S.A.” chants broke out often. When it all ended, and “Y.M.C.A.” played on the speakers, Trump and some of the competitors began to dance.
