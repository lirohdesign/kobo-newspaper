.DISCUSSION... Issued at 205 AM EDT Sat Aug 15 2026

Unfortunately, the next 36 hours will remain active with multiple waves of convection, each one impacting the next to some degree and leading to an overall low confidence forecast. The first round is already lighting up over central IL. Water vapor imagery shows a pair of robust MCV`s over the Central Plains and downstream, the LLJ is ramping up once again with a fire hose of low level theta-e advection (850mb dewpoints near 20C over central IL). This activity will continue to propagate eastward through the morning. The environment currently over our CWA is not favorable for deep, moist convection given a steady feed of light easterly low level flow locally. Of course the LLJ/moisture advection will modulate this to some degree but the LLJ will be in a weakening state after sunrise and midlevel lapse rates are very meager in our area. Do expect storms to impact our CWA roughly 11-17Z but should weaken with time and E/NE extent. Some gusty winds can`t be ruled out in this scenario, especially in our SW zones, but overall the severe risk looks low in our area with round 1. While these storms are expected to remain more progressive than recent days, the very moist environment could still support brief heavy rain and our southern counties really can`t take anymore at this point so flooding will be a concern. No changes to the flood watch planned at this time.

One possible scenario to watch for though is a delayed arrival tied to a more robust MCV as suggested by a few runs of the HRRR. If this occurs, and convection can get more rooted to the surface, then round 1 could feature a higher severe risk (as well as greater heavy rain/flooding concerns) in the 15-18Z window. While this remains a possibility, it is not the preferred solution at this time due to the poor initialization seen in those runs of the HRRR and the overall synoptic and mesoscale picture at this point.

Unfortunately, round 2 slated for late afternoon/evening will depend largely on the exact evolution of the first round and any potential subtle MCV`s ejecting out of upstream convection. An earlier/weaker round 1 increases the concern for round 2 and vice versa. If there is sufficient recovery time, MLCAPE values could rebuild to over 1000 j/kg and 40 kt winds at 700mb yield moderate 0-3km shear profiles. Organized convection will be possible in this scenario with the best chances likely east of I-69. Will have to watch the mesoscale environment closely today as models hint there could be greater than 200 m2/s2 of 0-1km helicity in the vicinity of this warm front, very low LCL`s, and just enough instability to yield a tornado threat. That is of course highly dependent on how morning convection plays out and it is equally plausible both instability and/or midlevel forcing fail, resulting in a complete null event. Very challenging scenario and requires close monitoring today.

Additional storms then expected late tonight/Sunday morning and possibly again Sunday afternoon. The story here is much the same with uncertainties compounding for each successive wave of convection. Current best guess is that another nocturnal strengthening of the LLJ will feature more storms with mainly a heavy rain threat roughly 08-14Z. At this point though, Sun AM convection looks weaker and more scattered than tonight (driven more by approaching midlevel trough/cold front than very strong moisture flux convergence). However, much like today it won`t take much to cause flooding in our south and the flood watch may need to be extended through Sun morning to account for this. Cold front finally passes late Sun afternoon. Exact timing and degree of instability recovery remain very much in question, but some strong storms may again be possible, especially south of US-24.

Relief finally arrives Sun night with dry conditions through Tue. Another cold front passes on Wed but this should be quick-hitting and most guidance suggests tranquil weather late next week into next weekend.

Good morning. There are good reasons to abandon books we’re not enjoying, but our attenuated attention might lead us to tap out too early.

By Melissa Kirsch

The book-tracking app Goodreads added a “Did Not Finish” (DNF) designation this spring so that readers can log books that they’ve put down or abandoned. The change, Erik Piepenburg reported in The Times, provoked an online debate: Is it a cop-out to DNF? As a reader who has been known to graze on books as if at a grand buffet, tasting a little bit of everything, I enter this discourse bashfully. I feel strongly that life’s too short to spend time on books one doesn’t like. But sometimes I wonder if the problem isn’t with the books but with me: impatient, seduced by novelty, impossible to satisfy.

When I told my friend Leigh that some people refuse to forsake books, she responded with outrage. “Why would you bother with a book you’re not enjoying? There are a million books,” she told me, as if I hadn’t sampled the first chapter of most of them. “This sounds like vestigial Puritanism.”

Indeed, but one needs to give a book a fair shake before deciding it would be punishing to continue. It takes a minute to engage with a book, to get a sense of the author’s voice and style, to understand what the book is doing. We like when books “grab” us, when they are “impossible to put down.” We’ve gotten used to the grabbiness of the internet, the posts and memes and videos that seize our attention and hold it hostage. I know I’m guilty of tapping out too early. I do it with books, but also with shows and movies. My attention struggles to distinguish between a novel and a TikTok video, between art and content, between being grabbed and being beckoned.

The most compelling reason to see a book through to the end is also the simplest: “It feels good to finish stuff,” one of the people Erik interviewed said. This is no small thing. Adding a book to my Completed list is incredibly satisfying. Even if I didn’t love the book, I’ve accomplished something. I’ve taken the journey the author intended. I’ve allocated my attention to something for a significant duration — a tiny modern miracle.

My friend Alice is one of the healthiest readers I know. She reads quickly and widely and is always ready with a recommendation. I figured she was a closer, a never-DNFer, the type to finish a book by hook or by crook.

“I could be 90 percent of the way through and I’ll dump it,” she told me, to my surprise. “I’ll dump anything! I’d let the fork fall from my mouth if I’m halfway through a dinner I don’t like.”

And yet Alice finishes more books than anyone I know. I never hear about the ones she abandons, because she does it quickly and without fanfare, without self-excoriation. She’s not keeping a public record of her DNFs, as Goodreads’s new feature encourages her to. Not finishing a book isn’t the same as never finishing a book. There’s no shame in putting aside a book that you don’t like, but finding a book that you do like takes commitment. You might try a few bites from the carving station or the chafing dishes, but eventually you sit down and eat a proper meal.

    ADVICE FROM WIRECUTTER

    Hacks for keeping small spaces organized

    If you live in a small space, you probably need to get creative to find enough storage for all your stuff. Don’t discount underutilized places: The backs of doors, for example, can be great places for miscellaneous items that fit into hanging organizers, like craft supplies, scarves or shoes. In the kitchen, use the backs of cabinet doors to hold lightweight items like aluminum foil or sponges. Hooks or magnets on the sides of the fridge can hold knives, paper towels or other small supplies. Finally, don’t overlook the forgotten areas of your home. The little nook between the toilet and the vanity, or the awkward upper corner of your kitchen, could be the perfect spot for random bits and bobs. — Caroline Mullen
