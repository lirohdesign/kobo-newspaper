02. nyt briefing

Good morning. The season’s new releases give us a cultural road map, but the most rewarding reading, listening and watching comes from the detours.

By Melissa Kirsch

It’s Labor Day weekend, and I’m here to remind you that summer’s not over, even though schools and scolds try to lop off the final two weeks, declaring a premature finish to a season that, as any equinoctial will tell you, extends until Sept. 22. Hang on. Linger in the post-7 p.m. sunsets while you can. Finish the book you started in August. Make it to the beach. Stay a little looser. The apples and sweaters and soups are standing patiently by.

I’m reluctant to let summer go because I’m reluctant to button up, to start rushing again. I associate summer with wandering, both literally and culturally. I was chatting recently with my colleague Minju about this season’s big films — “Spider-Man,” “The Odyssey,” “The Devil Wears Prada 2” — and we realized our most satisfying cinematic experiences were the detours those blockbusters sent us on. Minju was reminded how much she likes Samantha Morton, who played Circe in “The Odyssey,” which sent her to rewatch other films Morton starred in, like “Jesus’ Son” and “Minority Report.”

I loved “The Invite,” directed by and starring Olivia Wilde, and set off to watch her in Gregg Araki’s “I Want Your Sex” from July, and also to revisit her prior directorial efforts, “Don’t Worry Darling” and “Booksmart.” “Don’t Worry Darling” stars Harry Styles, who’s currently performing a 30-show residency at Madison Square Garden. I didn’t get tickets, but I’ve been dancing to his delightful 2022 album “Harry’s House” all summer.

Of course the omnivorous consumer follows her aesthetic bliss year round, but the expansive days of the warmer months lend themselves to cultural meandering.

Minju read Rachel Cusk’s new book “Life of M,” then wandered back to her earlier “Outline” trilogy, which she’d meant to read but never had. Olivia Rodrigo’s duet with Robert Smith on her new album led me back to Smith’s days with the Cure, “Kiss Me, Kiss Me, Kiss Me” and other albums from the 1980s.

I had been eagerly anticipating “Remake,” Ross McElwee’s documentary about making art after the loss of his son. Before watching it, I went back to revisit his 1986 film, “Sherman’s March,” knowing the new one referenced it and I’d want to have it fresh in my mind. After seeing “Remake,” which was stunning, I immediately cued up McElwee’s 2011 doc about his son, “Photographic Memory,” and set out to find out where his other family documentaries were streaming. My McElwee journey was long, winding and totally absorbing, a cinematic ramble.

I like the wisdom to always be planning your next trip or vacation, even if it’s far in the future, so you have something to look forward to. If the end of summer brings with it a little sadness, as it does for me, why not plan your cultural itinerary for the fall? Where will “Practical Magic 2,” a 28-years-later sequel starring Sandra Bullock, Nicole Kidman and Dianne Wiest, lead? To Bullock’s hilarious turn in “The Heat”? Wiest in “Edward Scissorhands” or perhaps off the path to her small role in “Footloose”? Barbara Kingsolver’s new novel “Partita” comes out Oct. 6. Will we be rereading “The Poisonwood Bible” and “Demon Copperhead” come Halloween?

The origin is the equinox, the destination is the solstice. You can choose your waypoints, but the detours and diversions will emerge once you’re on the road.

    By Melissa Clark

    Jalapeño Grilled Pork Chops

    Eric Kim’s jalapeño grilled pork chops are perfect for Labor Day weekend feasting — or for anytime you need an excuse to light up the grill. Soaked in a punchy marinade of cilantro, garlic, chiles and just enough sugar to encourage caramelization, the thin chops cook quickly, charring lightly at the edges. Eric tops them with a bright onion and cilantro relish and serves them with rice on the side. I’d add a ripe tomato salad to take full advantage of these last days of summer.

    ADVICE FROM WIRECUTTER

    Why you sleep better at hotels

    If you’ve ever returned from vacation and felt like you slept better in that dreamy hotel room, that’s by design. Hotels tinker with lighting, sound and temperature to help guests get the best sleep. But it’s possible to recreate some of those experiences in your own bedroom. Prioritize making your bedroom dark and quiet. Blackout curtains and white noise machines can help. Making the room cool is important, too: The ideal temperature is 65 to 68 degrees. Finally, hotel mattresses often have a luxe plush or pillow top. If you want to replicate that feeling without buying a whole new mattress, try a fluffy topper. And if you are ready to upgrade the mattress? Many of our favorites are on sale for Labor Day weekend. — Shaena Montanari

    U.S. OPEN

    Every year, I take time off work to go to the U.S. Open, New York City’s best event. Last week I watched the Olympic medalist Zheng Qinwen in her last qualifying match, as she scrambled to save the second set before a packed crowd. Rain eventually stopped play, and she smoothly took the third set the next day. I recommend watching her match this weekend, in addition to two others:

    Zheng Qinwen vs. Madison Keys: After becoming the first Chinese player to win singles gold at the Olympics, Zheng spent months recovering from injury and surgery. She squares off against Keys, an American who won the Australian Open last year.

    Taylor Fritz vs. Francisco Cerundolo: Fritz, the 2024 U.S. Open runner-up, is trying to become the first American man to capture the singles trophy in more than 20 years. Fritz appears in good form this summer after winning a lower-level tournament in Washington, D.C.

    Alexandra Eala vs. Iva Jovic: Eala, the first Filipino to win a title on the women’s tour, is an enormous crowd favorite. Her opponent today, Jovic, is also her good friend. (Jovic won both of their previous matches.)
