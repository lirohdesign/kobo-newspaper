.DISCUSSION... Issued at 131 AM EDT Fri Sep 25 2026

The energy from the early week rainfall deepens off the east coast today. Meanwhile, height falls ensue among the mid level ridging overhead as a shortwave shifts southward through Lower Michigan. Given how dry we`ve gotten, no precipitation is expected from the wave. Gradient winds weaken as the low level jet regroups over the east coast. This allows for light winds as well. Subsidence takes over behind the wave and dry air continues to remain across the area. Given a sort of rex block to our east along the east coast, dry weather is expected to prevail today into early next week. As a vorticity streamer in the central US runs into the block, it shears out and dampens during the weekend into early the next work week, further indicating the strength of the block. The low low level theta-e airmass slowly increases between today and early next week indicating that temperatures will be slowly rising after today. Highs in the upper 60s today, surpass 70F on Saturday and have a 40+% chance of achieving 75F south of US-30 starting Monday. Lows in the 40s are also expected to continue until we get to Tuesday night.

Warm advection finally begins to ensue later Tuesday into Tuesday night. The greatest chance of achieving 75F is on Tuesday as a result. However, this is still when medium range models differ on how much moisture to bring into the area and, therefore how warm it gets still comes with some uncertainty. This only muddies the thunderstorm chance picture from that late Tuesday time into Wednesday before chances reload for Friday and the weekend. Tuesday`s chance comes as a diffuse area of vorticity pushes eastward in association with a cold front. The wave is rather flat and much better forcing is farther north. The forcing for late week to possibly the weekend is a cutoff low in the southwest that gets pulled northward in the next trough. The ECMWF is notorious for leaving these waves in the southwest too long, but the GFS is notorious for moving systems too fast. The answer may lie somewhere in between.

02. nyt briefing

By Sam Sifton

Good morning. President Trump rolled out a literal red carpet for China’s president, Xi Jinping, who made his first visit to the White House in more than a decade. Talks between the two leaders continue today.

There’s more below, including the tale of a tradwife influencer who became an ex-tradwife influencer. But first, let’s talk about the best television of the 21st century.

What are the 100 best television shows of the 21st century? The Times polled hundreds of TV professionals and TV lovers and tabulated their answers. All week, we’ve been counting the list down. Today’s installment brings us the top 20, and if you think I’m going to tell you what tops the list, you’re out of your mind. No spoilers in The Morning! You need to see this list for yourself.

(Not a subscriber? Not a problem. Just download the Times app and you’ll have free access to the list — and to all our journalism — for a month.)

You’ll have thoughts about the choices. I know I do. American television culture has come a long way from rabbit ears and national conversations about what happened on “Dallas” last night. Among many other things, there’s so much more of it now: The number of original adult U.S. scripted series went from 182 in 2002, according to data from FX, to 600 in the peak production year of 2022. That’s a lot of TV.

And we’re much more fragmented now than we were in the 20th century, culturally and politically. It can be hard to find common ground. Viewers find niches. Some of your co-workers may have gathered in the coffee room to talk about “Fleabag,” but there were probably others beside them who’d never heard of the show. Maybe they were “Borgen” people or addicted to “RuPaul’s Drag Race.”

We’re all some kind of people, as my colleague Wesley Morris has said. My wife’s never seen “Succession,” which is a story about a world parallel to mine. I don’t believe I watched a single episode of “Gilmore Girls.” (I have watched all 380-something episodes of “Deadliest Catch.”) You’re going to see choices you agree with here, and ones you cannot fathom. (I’ll break the spoiler rule with one example: “Catch” did not make the list!)

Here’s one thing a lot of people will ask: “How in the name of gabagool is ‘The Sopranos’ not on the list?” The answer may sound pedantic, but rules are rules: The series started last century, in 1999. (So did “The West Wing,” “Freaks and Geeks” and “Family Guy.”) We considered only series you could watch entirely in this one.

James Poniewozik, our television critic, calls that the technical answer. “The aesthetic one,” he writes, “is that ‘The Sopranos’ is all over this list.” There are antiheroes everywhere in our ranking, alongside the textured human characters who replaced them when antiheroes went out of fashion.

Go explore the list. Send us your own ballot. See videos of stars talking about their picks. Luxuriate in — and contribute to! — the reader comments. And, finally, watch the folks behind the series we put first talk about their show. The whole package is a joy.

    Make Genevieve Ko’s recipe for caramelized garlic fish this weekend and it’ll reward you with a technique you can use midweek whenever you need a fast, flavorful meal. The sauce is bonkers: a funky, fish-sauced Vietnamese caramel that brings flaky white fish into a deep umami embrace. But not only flaky white fish (catfish, say, or cod). You can use it to great effect on cubed tofu, on nuggets of chicken thigh, on seared pork chops, probably a bedroom slipper. Five stars!
