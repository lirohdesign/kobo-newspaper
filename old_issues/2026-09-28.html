.DISCUSSION... Issued at 147 PM EDT Mon Sep 28 2026

Another day with a largely persistence forecast as midlevel ridge and associated surface anticyclone remain locked over the Great Lakes. New England upper low finally opens up and lifts toward the Canadian Maritimes tomorrow allowing our ridge to drift eastward and southwest return flow to increase by late in the day. One more clear/calm night first though with lows in the upper 40s to around 50F. Patchy fog is possible once again but crossover temps may be a little harder to exceed than previous nights. Not expecting any widespread, dense fog but nearly ideal radiational cooling always raises the possibility for some localized impacts as we have seen the past two days.

Attention then turns to the northern stream Pacific jet currently entering British Columbia and the upper low digging through California. The eastward migration of these two features will drive our sensible weather through the second half of the week. The initial cold front still looks to stall to our northwest on Wed as it becomes increasingly divorced from the leading midlevel shortwave and strong southwest flow develops in the Upper Midwest downstream of ejecting upper low. Can`t entirely rule out a stray shower in our far NW Wed evening but by and large expect one more day of dry and pleasant weather with highs near 80F. This front doesn`t really cross our area until late Thu, possibly extending into Fri, as we await the arrival of a secondary, much more potent northern stream trough. Good 850mb theta-e will be pumped into the region ahead of the southern stream upper low by Thu but surface dewpoints are still only in the low/mid 60s with poor lapse rates aloft yielding very meager instability profiles. This is especially true if precip holds off until Thu night as is currently trending. Some embedded thunderstorms certainly possible but severe weather is not expected. Marginal instability will aid rain coverage/intensity but without a strong convective element expect more of a widespread, moderate rain event. Rain amounts around an inch are likely for most with pockets of 1.5" possible. Given recent stretch of dry weather, our area should be able to tolerate that with minimal impacts. Still some uncertainty with the weekend forecast and whether or not this frontal boundary completely clears our CWA as remnant upper low/ moisture plume slowly eject eastward. No changes to concensus initialization here.

02. nyt briefing

By Sam Sifton

Good morning from rain-soaked New York City. In other storm news, Hurricane Polo is bearing down on Baja California Sur, in northwestern Mexico, with maximum sustained winds of 115 m.p.h.

There’s plenty more below. But first, let’s add some protein.

Nearly 90 percent of Americans have enough protein in their diets, according to a recent government report. That hasn’t stopped food companies from trying to sell us more, nor stopped consumers from buying it.

Some of my colleagues started keeping track of the counterintuitive sources of protein they saw in grocery stores and gas station markets, in warehouse clubs and bodegas, in chain restaurants and coffee shops. They found protein cookies and candies. Protein coffee and protein water. Protein vodka. Protein milk. Here were protein-enhanced Pop-Tarts, Uncrustables, Kraft Mac & Cheese, Cheerios. They saw protein-loaded meat sticks and brownie-batter protein balls, too. Protein chips and protein crackers.

A company that collects detailed data on retail food sales counts 37,232 products with protein claims on the front of their packaging. Those claims come in big fonts. And those big fonts draw attention. They work. The company says it’s measured around $90 billion in consumer spending on protein products over the last year, including more than $2 billion on protein pizzas. That’s up a lot, as the following chart shows:

Americans are way into protein. Why is that?

Selling points

The simple answer is hype, according to the reporters Margot Sanger-Katz and Larry Buchanan. If weight-loss advice shows up in your social-media algorithm, the importance of protein will be at the center of it. My dudes who lift weights swear by the stuff — and march around with four-pound bags of nuts in their backpacks. So do the biohackers who want to live past 100. People taking GLP-1 weight-loss drugs hear about the macronutrient from their doctors.

And the government’s latest MAHA-coded dietary guidelines increased the levels of recommended protein by more than 50 percent. Americans should “prioritize protein foods at every meal,” according to the guidelines.

The food industry is happy to oblige, even if the science says that too much protein can lead to problems, including heart disease, kidney issues and weight gain. They sell us yogurt and cheese as the answer. Eggs, too, and salmon and tilapia, beans and tofu. They wrap little salamis in packaging that declares them “protein packs.” Protein is everywhere.

It’s even in snacks: in Protein Nom Noms, for instance, and Doritos Protein. “You’ve got the claims sneaking into categories that don’t make a lot of sense,” said an executive at the company that monitors retail food sales.

For the bold

The food industry has always rolled with nutritional trends, which come and go. As my colleagues show, Doritos are a pretty cool case study. The intensely flavored tortilla chips were sold in the 1970s with bags touting “no preservatives!” Since then, Doritos have been labeled “cholesterol free,” “Light (⅓ less oil),” low calorie, low carbohydrate and, right now, “naked of dyes.” (It’s legitimately weird to see a nude Dorito, without its cloak of bright orange flavor chemistry.)

Special K cereal is another one, a reminder that what’s old can be new again. Seven decades ago, food scientists teamed up with researchers at the Harvard School of Public Health to develop it. The main selling point, right there on the cereal box: “HIGH-PROTEIN.”

Take a look at the whole article, which has incredible images of protein-mad food packaging and a neat feature that allows you to call up photographs of random protein products.

And use this calculator to see how much protein you actually need, based on your age and weight.

    RECIPE OF THE DAY

    There’s a great instruction in Melissa Clark’s recipe for crispy chicken thighs with cucumber yogurt, which she adapted from one developed by the Maine chef Devin Finigan. It’s to “work confidently” when you turn over the chicken crisped in the pan, so as not to tear the skin. Confidence in the kitchen is everything. Even if you’ve only been at this game a little while, it’s important to bring self-assurance to your actions at the stove. Let the fat render. Let the skin go golden. Let it release. And then turn the bird, sure in the knowledge that you’ve done your best. The seasoning and that fantastic cucumber yogurt will do the rest. You’ve got this.
