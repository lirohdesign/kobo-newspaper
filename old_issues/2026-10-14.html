.DISCUSSION... Issued at 326 AM EDT Wed Oct 14 2026

With a ridge across SE TX and surface high pressure in the SE CONUS, waves are forecast to ride atop the ridge and then work to break down the ridge during this mid to late week period. As such, the shortwave that helped to force the overnight showers and storms moves quickly eastward toward the northeast, which allows a cold front to slide southeastward into the Ohio River Valley pushing showers southeastward out of the area early this morning. The subsidence behind this wave is expected to suppress any substantial development behind it for the rest of the day, today. However, some isolated to scattered light rain cannot be ruled out, especially south of US-24. Meanwhile, an area of low pressure rides northward along the stalled front stretched down into the southern Plains and allows the portion of the front stalled to our southwest to ride northeastward as a warm front as early as late tonight into early Thursday. So, while dew points fall below 60F across the whole area tonight, they are expected to moisten back above 60F again Thursday afternoon and night. It is interesting to see a stronger wave on the ECMWF bring an inch of rain accums over 6hrs right up to our doorstep Thursday morning and this appears to be from some stronger large scale ascent. This large scale ascent continues through the area during the afternoon. This is different on the GFS as it splits the stronger area of large scale ascent into two separate smaller areas to the north and south and then the large scale ascent during the afternoon is more diffuse or non- existent. This provides some uncertainty on our marginal chances for severe weather Thursday afternoon/evening, especially on its location. Instability appears to get into areas south of US-24 on both the ECMWF and the NAM (500 J/kg or less), but not the GFS. Instability is elevated and the column looks very moist so this would likely be a heavy rain scenario with some potential for gusty to damaging wind if it is able to get through the inversion. Models handle the forcing during this period differently so placement of rain bulls-eyes and intensity of them are all over the place across the area. The GFS has nearly rain during the 00-06z timeframe Thursday night, whereas the ECMWF has a bulls-eye over 0.5" nearby.

Now, at the same time as an upper low washes out as it moves northeastward towards the area Thursday, a deep mid level trough dives southward and pushes the remnant energy northeastward quickly and swings through later Friday through Friday night. This is followed by a strong 1040 mb surface high diving into the southeastern US. So we quickly have rain (with minimal convective component by this point) overtaken by lake enhanced rain during the day Friday. NAM Bufkit soundings indicate delta-Ts as much as 20 to 30F degrees using lake temps around 62 degrees. Lake induced inversions rise up to 700 mb on these forecast soundings, which fairly incredible. Deeper moisture profiles arrive Thursday night and continue into early Friday morning before drier surface air begins to work in and cut off better rain formation. Given the strength of the surface high, much cooler air is able to enter the area and low temps Friday night and Saturday night will fall into the 30s. This turns the mind to frost possibilities, but Friday night looks like there will still be a bit of gradient around and probably the wind is still slackening at this point. Plus, we`ll still have lake clouds dissipating. A slightly better chance for frost occurs Saturday night, but we`ll have to see if winds are able to slacken enough at that point.

From Saturday night, flow across the CONUS appears to slacken and there is a period where nearly zonal flow is established until early the following weak as another deep trough enters the western CONUS. Because return flow becomes reestablished, expect a warming trend with a return to seasonable highs at least by Sunday, if not by Monday.

02. nyt briefing

By Sam Sifton

Good morning. The pope has lung cancer, the Vatican said. Joni Mitchell is now an American citizen. And there’s more news below, including an accounting of how you get your bananas.

Let’s start, though, with Russia’s war on Ukraine.

With winter bearing down, Russia is trying to destroy Ukraine’s energy grid and freeze the country into submission after more than four years of war. It’s also bombing civilians. Ukraine has struck back with attacks on Russian oil refineries and, recently, e-commerce warehouses across the nation. It has clawed back some territory, even though it’s running low on munitions.

But as the price of diesel fuel soars, President Trump sees the United States as collateral damage in Ukraine’s fight for survival. “He wants to make problems for the world,” Trump said of President Volodymyr Zelensky. “He wants to make problems for our farmers, our ranchers.”

Accordingly, Trump announced on Friday that the United States would suspend sanctions on Russian diesel. The announcement shocked Ukrainian and European leaders who have worked for years, with the help of the United States, to cut Russia off from the money that fuels its war. (Several Republican members of Congress objected yesterday, too. Many of them had just backed a new law to tighten the economic noose on Vladimir Putin, the Russian president. “This deal is a gift to our adversary and a betrayal of our allies,” they and some Democratic colleagues wrote.)

But it surely pleased Putin. If the agreement moves ahead, it could put some $4 billion of American money into the Russian economy. In return, Putin offered more war, launching an attack on the Ukrainian city of Zaporizhzhia that killed 22 people, including four children.

The U.S. midterm elections are only weeks away, and high fuel prices imperil Republican candidates. Buying Russian diesel might bring those prices down, Trump suggests. It would be hard to find a clearer example of his “America first” posture toward foreign policy.

On the ground

Russia’s escalating assaults on Ukrainian cities are taking an enormous toll. The same week as the strike on Zaporizhzhia, a Russian bomb hit a commuter bus in Kramatorsk, killing at least 30 people.

The Russian military has targeted civilians throughout the war. But the death toll is rising as Moscow deploys more “precision weapons” that have stretched Ukraine’s air defenses with a daily shower of attacks. Russia launched about 3,500 jet-powered drones against targets across Ukraine in September, reports Marc Santora, who has been covering the war since its start. In June, that number was 350. Ukraine is trying to engineer an interceptor that can stop them. (For now, F-16s try to shoot them down.)

The Russians use older weapons, too, including World War II-era “glide bombs” that can be stopped only by shooting down the fighter jets that deliver them, something the Ukrainian military lacks the ability to do. In July, the Russians dropped 8,300 of those.

A father’s grief

Siobhán O’Grady and Liubov Sholudko, reporting from Ukraine, told the story of one of those bombs, which detonated in the city of Sumy, in the country’s northeast, not far from the Russian border. Their account opens with a horrifying scene:

The 500-pound Russian bomb first appeared as a tiny blip on Yevhen Ovcharov’s live military map: a red speck gliding over the Ukrainian border and above the villages beyond.

Then he saw it hurtling toward his hometown, Sumy.

Yevhen was a Ukrainian soldier deployed 300 miles from home, on the eastern front. It was 9:45 p.m. on July 3 when he reached for his phone.

His wife, Nataliia Ovcharova, answered quickly. Don’t worry, she told him. She and the girls were fine and happily walking home from the park. Their 13-year-old, Veronika, was trailing slightly behind with a friend. Their youngest, Milana, who was 5, was beside her.

At 9:46, the call was cut off.

Yevhen called back once, twice, three times.

For six minutes, he heard nothing.

At 9:52, his phone rang. It was Veronika. She screamed that she was covered in blood. Milana, she said, was lying completely still on the ground.

“No one is helping,” she sobbed. “Dad, please come save us.”

Yevhen ran outside and jumped in a car to start the four-hour drive home.
