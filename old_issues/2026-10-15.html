.DISCUSSION... Issued at 245 AM EDT Thu Oct 15 2026

Surface high pressure slides eastward this morning across the northern Great Lakes. This allowed drier air to seep into the area behind the slowly southward-moving cold front from Wednesday. This front quickly turns around into a northward-moving warm front today as a deep upper low forces a surface low northward along the western extension of the baroclinic zone down to the Southern Plains. So, the attention turns to the timing of the surface warm sector`s arrival to see if we can get surface-based or only elevated convection. The GFS still has areas of large scale ascent moving through the area this afternoon whereas the ECMWF appears to have most of the better lift moving north of the area. The drier the low levels can get and maybe lesser cloud cover before sunrise would be better for instability formation. Indications in both the NAM and the HRRR are that the surface warm front waits until 2 to 4z to get into our southern 1 to 2 rows of zones, which would allow a short time period (1 to 3 hours?) for surface-based convection after that. The main ingredient of concern is the shear, which the HRRR indicates has around 45 kts of EBWD shear. Now, because the low levels up to 850 mb are essentially saturated, LCLs are sub-1000m, but instability is 500 J/kg or below. So, perhaps there could be some shallow supercells or cells with embedded rotation. With the moist PWATs around 1.8 inches, heavy rain rates could occur, but cell motions are rather fast likely limiting overall flood threat; that and the fact that we`ve been dry for so long. The other possibility would be a moisture loaded core- collapsing damaging wind threat, but that weak of instability may also limit that threat. The cold front slides through overnight meaning the rain threat is out of the area by 12z Friday.

The aforementioned upper low is squeezed northeastward out ahead of a diving upper low from Canada. On it`s back side is a strong 1040 mb surface high indicative of relatively cold air that its bringing with it. This brings in a couple of hazards that we`ll get to describing here including lake effect rain, gusty winds, and potentially a frost chance. For the lake effect rain, the lake needs to get some instability going first as well as low level convergence and a return of low level moisture behind the cold front. Northwest wind convergence combines with these features around 12z Friday and would expect the intensity of lake enhancement to increase through around 18z when the vigorous vort max swings through. Given lake temps of 62 degrees, BUFKIT NAM soundings indicate 15 to 25 degree delta T values with tall lake induced inversions during the afternoon. However, there are indications that dry air begins to work into the low levels during the evening hours after the low level trough moves through and trajectories go more northerly. Low level moisture moves out of the area and there are indications that rain subsides by 12z Saturday, but could see lake effect clouds lingering Saturday morning, especially in the west. Then, northwest winds swinging through Friday evening gust between 30 and 40 mph, highest along the surface trough, which could bring down a stray 45 mph gust. Now, the cold air coming in Friday night has 850 mb temps down to -2C 12z Saturday morning before warming ensues. The pressure gradient slackens overnight into early Saturday morning and cloud cover also dissipates overnight bringing some questions about how scattered out clouds can be east of US-31. Given forecast lows securely in the 30s Friday night, we`ll have to watch for frost chances then, but wind and cloud cover complicate chances. A dry day continues on Saturday and surface high pressure overhead continues to keep the area calm wind-wise. Which allows a second opportunity for frost Saturday night as the core of the cold air lifts northeastward. Forecast lows dip down into the 30s again, but an increased gradient attempts to enter from the north. This appears to be the only complication for another frost attempt Saturday night.

Looking ahead, even over the next 2 to 3 days, anomalous high pressure building near Greenland causing blocking to build peaks during the 16th to 21st of October. This is about the time that the NAO/AO dips negative. Meanwhile, the aforementioned baroclinic zone has pushed eastward potentially stalling along the eastern seaboard and forming an upper low or continuing to push eastward. Either way, this allows a sprawling area of high pressure to remain across the area until at least around Thursday of next week and potentially even longer keeping dry air overhead.

02. nyt briefing

By Sam Sifton

Good morning. Let’s start today in the Lone Star State.

There are a number of tight races in the coming midterm elections, but there isn’t a more expensive one than the Senate showdown in Texas. It pits James Talarico, the Democratic nominee, against Ken Paxton, the state’s conservative attorney general. More than half a billion dollars has been spent on advertising already, and the race could become the most costly nonpresidential contest in U.S. history.

Democrats are hopeful, because Talarico has a tiny lead in the polls. But “such optimism stands in defiance of the political axiom that Texas is where idealistic Democrats go to be mugged by reality,” writes Robert Draper, a three-decade veteran of Texas political reporting. The party has not won a statewide race there for 32 years.

The race may turn on who each man says he — and his opponent — is.

Talarico has introduced himself to the state’s 32 million residents as a good Christian member of the Texas House, with a record of bipartisanship. At a town hall meeting last night, Talarico spoke about his faith and appealed to undecided voters. “I’m a proud Texas Democrat, but I already have a religion and I already have a sports team,” he said. “So, I have no problem sharing hard truths with my party if it’s what’s best for Texas.”

His appeal is meant to be broad, with proposals to cap credit card rates, cut middle-class taxes, lower prices and assist people with their first down payment on a home.

In return, Paxton and his supporters paint Talarico as a “weird” radical who believes that “God is nonbinary” and that there are more than two biological sexes. (Talarico did say those things but later renounced them.) Vice President JD Vance, stumping in Texas on Tuesday, mocked Talarico — whose girlfriend is a former aide — with a gay joke. “I love you too sir,” Vance told a man in the crowd. “In a totally platonic way, to be clear. I’m the vice president, not James Talarico.”

It gets uglier. Recently, Talarico disappeared from the campaign trail for nine days. He and his aides said it was a bad case of the flu. His conservative opponents pounced, spreading a rumor that he had been at a mental health facility in Ohio, a state Talarico said he had never visited.

That story proved false — The Times obtained time-stamped security camera images showing that Talarico spent a week at home — but the episode offered a lesson for the age of misinformation, my colleague Bayliss Wagner reported: “It is politically risky for a candidate, even one too ill to campaign, to disappear for nine days.”

There’s a reason Paxton and his allies have clawed at every possible advantage. He’s got baggage to spare. During a quarter-century in Texas politics, he has never lost an election. But as attorney general, he was charged with felony fraud. He cut a deal to drop the charges in exchange for paying restitution and performing community service. Then, a group of his aides accused him of bribery and abuse of office. The Texas House impeached him in 2023. (The State Senate acquitted him.) And last year, his wife, Angela Paxton, a state senator, filed for divorce on “biblical grounds,” after his impeachment trial revealed that he’d used burner phones, a shared Uber account and secret email address to conduct an affair.

No wonder her appearance in a new ad doesn’t mention Paxton’s name, but simply urges people to vote for the Republican ticket.

And Trump, for his part? His endorsement was strangely backhanded: “He may not dress right. He may not talk exactly perfectly, and he may not be the best-looking guy I’ve ever seen,” Trump said last month. “But you know what he is? He’s the greatest attorney general in America.”

Jess Bidgood, who writes our On Politics newsletter, had a good line about that:

It’s never great when the two people most crucial to your political survival struggle to simply say nice things about you.

It’s worse when one of those people is the president, and the other is your estranged wife.
