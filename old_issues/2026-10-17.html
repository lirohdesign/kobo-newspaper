.DISCUSSION... Issued at 304 AM EDT Sat Oct 17 2026

Surface high pressure continues to build in causing low level moisture to dissipate, lake induced instability to wane, and low level trajectories to become more southerly. As lake enhanced rain dissipates early this morning, dry air takes over and we`re in this dry pattern for the foreseeable future. This is as the upper jet retreats into MI and upper ridging builds into areas east of the Rockies. An area of low pressure washes out between Wednesday and Thursday as a cold front pushes southeastward ahead of another surface high. The ECMWF has rain approaching the area, but that`s really the only model that does so.

Otherwise, the temperatures are the most noteworthy piece of weather that should be discussed here. Morning temps fell back into the 30s this morning, but lingering wind and limited decoupling restricted frost formation to patchy at best despite temperatures falling into the mid 30s and even some low 30s in some outlying spots. 850 mb temps this morning reached around -2C and, even as that aforementioned return flow takes over, we have our lowest temperatures of this young cool season as freezing temperatures look to settle in along and south of US-24. The give and take between surface high pressure-affected weak flow and encroaching strengthening gradient to our north and west once again lessens confidence on frost formation on the northern side of our freeze watch for Sunday AM. There are signs in forecast soundings of a decoupling boundary layer Sun AM and again Mon AM, although lesser confidence in frost occurs Monday and even lesser for Tuesday. While WAA doesn`t appear to be established in the following days, temperatures are forecast to trend warmer, rising above 40 degrees by Wednesday AM. Highs surpass 70 degrees again Tuesday morning, although a few areas in White, Pulaski, Cass, and Fulton could achieve 70F Monday as well.

02. nyt briefing

Good morning. We’re all temporary citizens of makeshift communities created by happenstance, tiny disposable societies forged and dismantled in jury rooms and departure lounges.

By Melissa Kirsch

I resisted the urge to sneak a peek at the questionnaire of the man sitting beside me at jury duty a few weeks ago. He’d been totally unknown to me a half-hour before, but now, through the accident of being summoned to serve this civic duty on the same day, he was part of my world, an object of curiosity, my neighbor for the day. Who was this 30ish man in a plaid flannel who didn’t crack a smile when the hammy lawyer released a fellow juror with the quip, “Always the bridesmaid, never the bride”? Who were any of these people who, as I had, took the day off work and stepped out of the currents of their lives to enter this fluorescent-lit parallel universe of judgment and justice?

Jury duty presents an acute instance of the makeshift community: strangers who come together for a short period, and in that time become their own little society with its own customs and laws, its own characters and gossip. These accidental communities happen all around us, all the time: the people sitting around you on the beach, fellow passengers on a flight, other fans who started queuing for concert tickets before dawn. Unlike when, say, staying in the same bed-and-breakfast or sharing a cubicle at work, socializing is not expected; but just by being present, you’re part of the play.

How quickly people go from invisible to mattering! At jury duty, the woman who works at a nonprofit and grew up in Kansas has such thoughtful answers to the lawyers’ queries: She is the most beloved of all my new neighbors. We share a smile when we’re released for lunch, the pop-up community equivalent of best friendship. And that man in the cap watching a movie on his phone: He’s breaking the no-devices rule, and we all revile him.

By afternoon, we have familiarized ourselves with routes from the metal detector to the assembly room, assembly room to bathroom: These are our neighborhood’s streets. We all know the same sleepy security guard, the no-nonsense courthouse staff; they live here too. Our outside lives become dim — do we really have jobs and spouses and full-fledged personalities? Our friends have no idea about these people to whom we’ve uttered not a word but have somehow become our confederates, no inkling of all the infrastructure we’ve erected.

Then, as quickly as this society materialized, it evaporates. Those of us who have not been empaneled are abruptly dismissed at 4:30 p.m. There is no duty keeping us here, no actual ties to unravel, so we spill out the doors and stagger back to our actual friends and neighbors. We will probably not remember one another, and isn’t there a tiny bit of grief in that? How strange to periodically take up residency in these disposable towns, to participate in their bureaucracies so intensely and then never revisit them. We return home to our established relationships, the people we’ve lived beside for years, those who know us and whom we know. How wonderful to be recognized, depended upon, to contribute to the ongoing project of building a community we hope will last.

    ADVICE FROM WIRECUTTER

    Surprising things you should refrigerate

    Of course, your milk and poultry need to be kept cool. But the chilly darkness of your fridge can also help many common household objects last much longer. Cold temperatures can prevent an open bottle of super glue from turning rock solid or slow the aging process of unused photo film. Same goes for expensive skin care products, like vitamin C serums. Use teeth-whitening strips? The cold helps delay the breakdown of hydrogen peroxide and other active ingredients. Just make sure any nonfood items are clearly labeled, out of sight or out of reach from anyone who might not understand that they are not safe to ingest. — Maki Yazawa
