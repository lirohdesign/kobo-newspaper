Epoch AI — Gradient Updates & Data Insights

2 days ago · synthesized 2026-10-26

Oct 26: Epoch's 'We tried to automate Epoch' reports that its Automation Reports, tasks Epoch staff do regularly (graphic design, planning research projects), were scored against the rubric items a human employee would be judged on. The leaders, Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra, averaged 65%; 100% would mean the task can be handed over, so every task would have been rejected or revised from a human. AIs did better on clearly defined work (coding, data analysis) than on open-ended judgment. GPT-6 Astra set a pilot experiment's token budget too low, so 22% of runs returned no answer, then treated that as a finding about the models. One task has already crossed the threshold and moved to AI. Epoch cautions 65% does not mean 65% of the way to automation. Read from the feed's summary of the longer report; who wrote the rubrics, and when, is in the report and was not read. Oct 9 InnovationEval (best result 15% of SDPO's gains) is the earlier reading. Inequality & capital ownership

Washington Center for Equitable Growth (monthly digest)

5 days ago · synthesized 2026-10-23

Oct 23: Equitable Growth assesses Sen. Wyden's Stability and Opportunity for Workers Act: a 26-week minimum UI duration at 75% wage replacement, automatic Extended Benefits of up to 52 weeks when national unemployment rises, lower earnings thresholds, a federal job-seekers allowance, and 26 weeks of benefits for full-time training. It reports the share of unemployed workers receiving benefits fell from over 50% in the 1950s to under 30% recently, and Florida and Arizona replace under 15% of average weekly wages. It also says the research on AI's labor market effects is mixed and gives no measured AI job-loss figure, so the AI case for the bill rests on scenario planning. Earlier, Oct 5: an argued piece on the budget (FY2026 revenue just under 17% of GDP, spending about 23%).
