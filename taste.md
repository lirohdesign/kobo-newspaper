# Taste — what counts as signal for the Reddit digest

This is the rubric the daily AI filter/summarizer uses to decide what belongs
in the digest and how to present it. It comes directly from conversation about
what you actually use Reddit for, corrected against an independent profile
built from six years of Libby borrowing history (that profile leaned hard into
its own project's lens — some of its calls are overridden below).

## What you actually use Reddit for
- Market sentiment and trade conditions — what's happening on the ground, from
  people doing the work, not analysts or pundits
- Recommended reading and films — discovery, not reviews-as-content
- Response to policy, from people it actually affects
- NOT abstract discourse, ideological argument, or performance-of-being-smart

## Comments over posts
In most relevant communities the post is a prompt — often a photo or a
question — and the value is in the discussion that follows. Treat the OP as
context (title + a one-line anchor is usually enough) and surface the
substantive replies: the practitioner explaining *why*, the correction, the
on-the-ground report. Drop one-liners, "this," "lol," and noise that's just
replying to a reply.

## Photo/video doesn't survive the trip to Kobo
Image- and video-heavy posts won't render usefully through Instapaper on an
e-ink screen. Don't skip these threads outright — the comments underneath may
still be the whole point — but don't try to describe the image, and don't pull
a thread whose *entire* value is visual.

## The daily buckets
1. **Trades, on-the-ground practice, emerging tech** — aviation, drones,
   building, small-scale agriculture, tools and repair. Practitioner-level
   discussion of what's actually happening, changing, breaking.
2. **Market sentiment & real estate economics** — builder/contractor
   "temperature reads," trade conditions, what people closer to the ground are
   seeing. Filter out petty gripes and consumer-complaint threads — the
   temperature read is the signal, the rant is not, even though they cluster
   in the same subs.
3. **Climate, ecology, conservation, climate justice, human rights** — high
   interest, deliberately wide net. These topics attract outrage-shaped
   threads as readily as anything else, so the slugfest filter below applies
   here at least as strongly.
4. **Political economy — academic register, not the slugfest** — land and
   ownership, Georgist and structural framings, distributional (not aggregate)
   data discussion. The interesting version argues from evidence and
   structure; the uninteresting version picks a side and yells. Filter hard
   for the former, and don't mistake topic overlap for fit.
5. **Sci-fi book and film recommendations** — a real discovery channel for
   you. Watch specifically for recommendation threads ("what should I
   read/watch next," "looking for something like X"), not general sci-fi
   discourse or news.

## Seasonal / triggered segments — not daily
Some of the best material clusters around calendar events rather than showing
up steadily, and forcing it into a daily rotation would mostly produce empty
runs punctuated by floods:
- **Booker Prize and Nobel Prize in Literature** — longlist/shortlist/winner
  windows for the Booker across the year, and the Nobel announcement each
  October. Explicitly **not** the Pulitzer.
- **Major reports** — IPCC assessment report releases, significant economic
  reports (major data drops, Fed releases) — and the discussion they spark.

These should be checked for around their known windows (or matched on
keywords like "Booker," "Nobel," "IPCC") rather than pulled from daily.

## What to filter out entirely, not just deprioritize
- **"Lottery ideology" content** — FIRE, get-rich-quick, hustle-culture,
  personal-finance-bro material. You've described this as specimens to argue
  *against*, not material to be served to you. Exclude, don't deprioritize.
- **Culture-war slugfests** — outrage-shaped threads: high comment velocity,
  low structural content. You're hunting the seam between positions, not
  the fight itself.
- **AI hype/doom cycles** — the daily-news churn about AI is out. Rare,
  genuinely significant AI events (the "Claude source code leak" kind of
  story) will reach you through the Guardian/NYT sources already in this
  pipeline — this digest doesn't need to try to also catch them, and trying
  to would mostly just import the noise.
- **Petty gripes** — individual complaint threads in trade, real-estate, and
  hobby subreddits. Same principle as #2 above: the temperature read is
  signal, the individual rant is not.

## Format note
A Kobo-via-Instapaper digest is a long-form reading object, read in
sustained-concentration mode rather than skimmed like a phone feed. Bias
toward long, structurally-argued, evidence- or experience-bearing threads over
high-velocity hot takes — that's not just taste, it's matching the source
material to the only mode in which it will actually get read.
