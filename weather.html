.DISCUSSION... Issued at 238 AM EDT Fri Sep 4 2026

Another murky forecast period lies ahead with respect to thunderstorms today. First, the heat advisory was expanded once again to encompass remaining northern Indiana counties and a majority of northwest Ohio counties (exempt are Williams and Fulton). Michigan is also exempt. Heat indicies once again approach 100F this afternoon. Observations on Thursday showed some sites reaching heat advisory criteria by noon, if not the 11am hour. High clouds this morning could briefly temper heat indicies, but afternoon sun could easily flip the script. While duration of advisory criteria may be limited today in some areas, we can then play the consecutive-days card as we stare down the prospect of our 4th consecutive very hot day.

Ongoing this morning is an increasingly mature area of thunderstorms over central WI. Forecast guidance generally suggests this cluster will weaken as it crosses into western Michigan after sunrise. However, the instability gradient could lead any lingering storms into southern Michigan while effect shear of 40 knots could aid in thunderstorm maintenance. This is at odds with lacking lapse rates less than 7 C/km. Confidence is low on how these storms evolve for the remainder of the morning, but they will be monitored closely prior to sunrise.

Like 24 hours ago, our marginal risk of severe thunderstorms was upgraded to a slight risk this morning. This is primarily driven by the anticipated development of thunderstorms this evening amid a strengthening 500-mb jet in which we lie within the right-entracne region. The low-level jet strengthens as well. Timing is nearly a toss up in this challenging, nuanced mesoscale environment, yet sometime after 8pm EDT appears probable for storms to redevelop. Confidence is low, but damaging wind gusts and hail would be the primary hazards from any severe storms today and tonight.

Drying out Saturday in the wake of any early-morning lingering thunderstorms and not quite as hot as the upper-level jet buckles. By Sunday, Canadian high pressure seeps in from the northeast, shunting the upper-level ridge out of our area for a time and bringing us a period of dry weather as well. Look for surface low pressure to cross the Canadian Prairie midweek, with a cold front tracking across the northern and Central Plains. This cold front looks to bring rather uniform coverage of showers and thunderstorms to our area around midweek. Beyond Wednesday, there is varying magnitudes of our well-discussed upper-level ridge, now displaced through the mid-Mississippi Valley. This ridge may build back north bringing continued warmth, or, the cold front could hang-up atop this ridge keeping us in line for additional late-week storms.
