.DISCUSSION... Issued at 552 AM EDT Sat Sep 12 2026

A warm front near Indy as of this writing will continue to lift northeastward through the day, reaching our area later this morning around 12-15z, then heading into Southern Ontario/Lake Erie by around 18z. Warm air/moisture advection is forcing an area of rain and thunderstorms near the front, and this will lift into our area for the morning. Was initially suspicious the thunder would not make it here, but as of 9z it looks fairly healthy thanks to some elevated instability. As the warm front moves north, moisture transport takes on a more east-northeasterly track-so have the best chances along and south of US 24 (best east of I 69) through the morning/early afternoon. Meanwhile, an upper level trough/shortwave and surface cold front approaches from the west. As of this writing, the boundary extends southward from western Lake Superior into West- Central WI/Eastern MN and IA/IL. Models don`t seem to have a great handle on the precipitation associated with this feature (many are dry at this time in the latest runs). Its fairly light, but there are a couple thunderstorms near the IL/WI line. The actual upper level trough and surface front look to reach our western CWA border by 00z (right along Lake MI shore, extending from IL into northern Ontario). Some of the models indicate a pre-frontal trough possibly developing towards the afternoon, which could re-invigorate the already moistened atmosphere in areas mainly along/south of US 24. I suspect as the better moisture transport gradually diminishes and shifts more E-NE out of our area late this morning that we`ll see a diminishing trend to the precipitation along the incoming cold front (thus keeping areas further north and west of US 24 dry with variable cloud cover). In the afternoon, the shortwave/pre-frontal trough is likely to trip an increase in shower/storm activity again, especially as it arrives at a time of decent heating (sfc cape potentially up to 1500 J/kg). Luckily we don`t have much in the way of any shear or steep mid level lapse rates, so not expecting any severe weather at this time.

Otherwise, heating up into the mid-upper 80s for many locations today (especially further northwest where cloud cover is lower/no precipitation). Expect dry conditions tonight as the cold front moves through, with winds shifting west-northwest. While winds overnight are lower initially, they increase out of the N-NW Sunday morning into the afternoon, especially near Lake MI and along/north of the toll road. If winds on Lake MI increase to 15kts sustained and gust to around 20-25 knots...we could end up seeing waves of 3-5 feet on Lake MI. However, the past few runs of models have flip flopped on the intensity of these winds (initially lighter with 2-3 ft waves). Opted to hold off on the beach hazards statement this issuance, but did maintain a high swim risk forecast for both La Porte/Berrien County beaches. If stronger winds verify, waves should reach 3-5 feet fairly quickly, approaching at angles of 45 to 90 degrees relative to shore with 6-7 second periods. This would be ideal for strong rip and structural currents, and a strong southward flowing longshore current. If heading to Lake MI this weekend, make plans for out of the water activities. I suspect the confidence for this forecast will be higher by this afternoon.

Rain and storms are possible again on Sunday afternoon/evening as another trough swings through and the cold front potentially gets hung up to our south and another weak trough moves in before high pressure builds in from the northwest. Have chances (20-30%) mainly west of IN-15, then east of IN 15 chances focus further south of US 30 where the front could get hung up. Highs Sunday will be in the mid-upper 70s, low 80s.

Monday will be cooler and dry, with highs in the 70s. Sfc high pressure/an upper level ridge builds into the Great Lakes briefly, then by Mon night into Tue the surface high drifts east and the upper level ridge takes on a SW to NE orientation (extending into the lower Great Lakes and the NE CONUS). We`ll be on the periphery of this ridge, and as an upper low/surface low move through Ontario, it brings a trough and cold front eastward into our forecast area. There is uncertainty as to whether the precipitation chances arrive Monday night or closer to Tuesday afternoon. Either way, expect the front and precipitation/storm chances to cross from west to east through late Mon PM into Tuesday. However, the front does appear to stall out as it is limited by subsidence from the staunch upper ridge still lingering. This would result in a boundary over our forecast area that may linger into Thursday or Friday. For now, have 20-50% chances Tuesday (mainly north/west of US 24), then increasing to 30-70% by Tuesday night into Thursday (40-60%). There will be chances for thunderstorms. At this point, severe weather is not expected. High temperatures by Tuesday could reach back into the mid- upper 80s, but it will depend on the extent of cloud cover and precip.
