.DISCUSSION... Issued at 1226 PM EST Fri Nov 20 2026

Surface high pressure centered over the Appalachian Mountains continues to provide tranquil, dry weather for the next 48 hours or so. Our mild weather pattern persists. High temperatures have been 15-20 degrees above normals and lows about 5-10 degrees above normal the past few days. With southerly low level flow and WAA, temperatures are expected to remain in the mid to upper 60s for highs today and Saturday. This is abnormal for this time of year, as daily records for today (November 20th) and Saturday (November 21st) are around 70 degrees!

As high pressure gets pushed eastward Saturday due to influence from a trough over the Four Corners region, rain chances return on Sunday as a cold front approaches. A low pressure system will traverse the Upper Midwest, with a trailing attendant cold front sweeping through the Great Lakes. There are 60-80% chances for rain, however, the day will not be a washout. Scattered, light showers will move through the area at times on Sunday during the day. Rainfall amounts are expected to be light (up to 0.10") as showers outrun the better low level moisture and lift with the actual front. It will be breezy on Sunday with a tightening pressure gradient in the vicinity of the front; Wind gusts will be between 20-25 mph with a shift in direction from southwest to northwest. With the rain chances primarily in the afternoon and evening, there should be sufficient diurnal heating for temperatures to climb into the low to mid 60s for highs on Sunday, especially in NW Ohio.

Following the frontal passage, CAA and northerly flow ensues with temperatures moderating back towards seasonable levels with highs in the upper 30s to low 40s. As a high pressure system spills into the Great Lakes region from central Canada, the forecast is leaning cooler and dry for early into middle part of next week. The GFS and ECMWF are in solid agreement next week of 850 mb temps below 0C through the early part of next week. This should result in highs in the low 40s and lows in the mid to upper 20s for several days. Given that a strong El Nino is ongoing, a more active pattern may develop across the southeastern US with the jet stream keeping activity suppressed to the south. There is some indication in the long range guidance of a `Miller A` type system developing mid to late next week, but a lot of uncertainty remains. It is plausible we get some scattered rain/snow showers at times by mid to late next week, but there are no real signs of winter yet. An early look at Thanksgiving has highs near 40 degrees with 20-30% chances for scattered rain/snow showers.
