.DISCUSSION... Issued at 203 AM EDT Sun Jul 26 2026

Continued WAA and a moist airmass moving in from the west will make for a hot and humid Sunday. Highs will be in the upper 80s to near 90 degrees with dewpoints in the low to mid 70s. Mid level stratus are expected to persist through at least daybreak this morning, with skies gradually clearing by the afternoon. Skies may appear hazy at times as smoke from ongoing wildfires in the Pacific Northwest drifts in but remains elevated. No impacts to air quality or visibility are expected.

The hot and humid conditions Sunday and Monday will fuel an unstable environment for storms around the periphery of a building upper level ridge over the central US. Severe weather is possible late tonight into early Monday morning, then again Monday afternoon through Monday night. 00z and 06z CAM guidance is still split on what exactly will occur, but the consensus is that timing is trending slightly later on the first possible round of storms tonight. Some of the CAMs have storms entering our area as late as daybreak Monday. We will have to monitor upstream convection this evening in Wisconsin; if an MCS forms, this complex is expected to move southeast overnight into early Monday along the edge of the upper level ridge. Whether it maintains its strength into our area is uncertain, hence a broad Marginal Risk (level 1 of 5) for severe weather that has been maintained by SPC. If any storms do make it into our area, damaging winds would be the main threat.

Better chances for severe weather exist on Monday with a Slight Risk (level 2 of 5) and potentially more favorable diurnal timing. The environment will be similar Monday with a very unstable airmass overhead due to the heat and humidity. Should there be storms in the area Monday morning, lingering cloud debris could certainty delay the timing but for now thinking severe weather possible within a broad window of 2 PM EDT Monday to 2 AM EDT Tuesday. With up to 3000- 4000 J/kg SBCAPE, 30-50 kts of shear, and 7-8 C/km mid level lapse rates, storms are expected to grow upscale into a squall line or mature MCS and propagate south/southeast through our forecast area. Damaging winds will once again be the main threat. A brief embedded tornado or two will be possible as well as 0-1 SRH values approach 150-200 m2/s2 Monday evening into Monday night ahead of the line.

Tuesday and beyond will see a reprieve in the active weather pattern as high pressure builds over the Great Lakes. Dry and seasonable with highs in the mid 80s midweek. Intermittent rain/storm chances by the end of the week.

Additionally, as winds shift to become northwesterly on Tuesday, dangerous swimming conditions are expected to develop for southeast Lake Michigan beaches on Tuesday. Waves of 3 to 5 feet Tuesday morning will build to 7 to 10 feet by Tuesday afternoon/evening. With direct onshore flow, strong structural, rip, and longshore currents are expected. Structural currents will be especially strong on the north side of piers. Stay out of the water and stay off of the piers! Beach Hazards Statements will likely be needed in the coming days for La Porte County, IN and Berrien County, MI.&&
