.DISCUSSION... Issued at 557 AM EDT Sun Sep 13 2026

A cold front is crossing our forecast area this morning, draped from roughly southern Ontario into areas just southeast of Defiance, OH, Fort Wayne, and Marion, IN as of this writing. Behind the front we have light north-northwest flow, except near Lake MI where winds are picking up slightly near the lakeshore. Have some patchy fog around, mainly along/ahead of the front at this time. Visibilities at KGWB/KUSE/KOWX/KAOH are all less than 1 mile (several less than 1/2 mile), and expect this to persist through around sunrise or until the front moves through. Issued an SPS for patchy dense fog until 13z for areas roughly along/south of US 24 east of I 69.

Otherwise, northwest winds strengthen through the afternoon as surface high pressure builds in rapidly behind the exiting cold front. Gusts up to around 20-25 mph are expected, especially near Lake MI and north of US 30. Accordingly, waves on Lake Michigan around 2 feet this morning should build to 3 to 5 feet by early afternoon, persisting into late this evening. Waves approach at a 45- 90 degree angle relative to shore, with wave periods around 6 seconds. Expect a strong north to south flowing longshore current along the beach, and strong structural currents near piers and breakwalls (especially on the north side). Rip currents are expected. Those visiting the beach should plan to stay out of the water. Maintained the previously issued small craft advisory/beach hazards statement from late this morning into the late evening hours.

Outside of a light shower along/near the front this morning, we`ll remain dry until this afternoon/evening when a shortwave moves through aloft. Showers and maybe a couple thunderstorms develop near an fgen band along a frontal zone in in the 850-700mb range over IL/NW IN that drifts southeastward and weakens with time this afternoon/evening. Could see a rumble of thunder if we get steeper lapse rates around 6-7C/km in mid levels. Otherwise, much of the surface instability is to our southeast (near Lima, OH and along southern border), so don`t expect any widespread t-storm development.

Depending on the speed of the wave, precipitation/storm chances end by 00-3z along our southernmost border in IN/NW OH.

High pressure centers over the Great Lakes region then tonight into Monday night, shifting eastward gradually beneath an amplifying upper level ridge. The broad high initially is centered over the SE CONUS, and builds straight north into Ontario at 00z Tuesday, then begins to tilt on the north end towards the eastern seaboard through Wed/Thu, which will bring us into a more active pattern in terms of showers and storms. Models have an upper level low/surface low traversing the northern plains and Canadian Prairies, eventually reaching Hudson Bay by Tuesday night and onto Quebec by Wednesday afternoon. The upper level trough begins to breakdown the northwest periphery of the ridge as the low moves eastward, bringing a sfc cold front into our forecast area by Tuesday afternoon/evening. Decent warm air advection/moisture pumping into the area ahead of the front beneath the upper level ridge provides us with an unstable environment. The cold front has an ETA of 18-00z depending on the model, which would be favorable timing for maximizing available instability (Nam has 1500-2500 J/kg ML CAPE around this time). Bulk shear is on the order of 30-40 knots, with the best values further north in MI. SPC has our forecast in a marginal risk for severe weather accordingly. Mid level lapse rates aren`t all that good (around 6.5C/km max), and if the front arrives later it won`t be as favorable instability wise. If we do have severe weather, the main threats would be damaging winds. High temperatures Tuesday will be in the mid-upper 80s for most areas, with locations west of I 69 south of US 30 potentially reaching the low 90s. Coolest in lower MI, where temps will be in the lower 80s. Heat indices along and west of I 69, south of US 30 could reach around 100 degrees.

The cold front will move into our area and stall out, models appear to initially stall it out generally along US 24 by Wednesday. As the upper level trough moves north, the ridge builds back in again and could force the boundary further north-orienting along the toll road. Either way, wherever it sets up we`ll see favorable conditions for shower and thunderstorm activity. Flow becomes more zonal, which will help kick off any shower/t-storms through Thursday/Friday. Have the best chances (70-80%) late Tue afternoon (far west) into Wednesday morning-but expect values around 40-60% through Thursday. Thunderstorms are possible at times in this period. Highs will drop into the mid 70s and low-mid 80s Wed-Fri, then we`ll be in the 70s by the weekend.
