.DISCUSSION... Issued at 301 AM EDT Sun Aug 9 2026

Plenty of uncertainty in the forecast today, yet, depending on which solution verifies, heavy rainfall may prove the most likely hazard this evening along the Indiana-Michigan state line. This is in addition to the damaging wind gust and tornado risk from severe thunderstorms this afternoon (low confidence) and overnight (medium confidence).

As it goes here in northern Indiana, rounds of storms are highly dependent on how any convection earlier in the day evolves. Early this morning, the area to watch is over the ND-SD state line where a growing complex of storms exists. This complex drifts east and into northeast IL and into IN this afternoon. Preexisting high pressure may be the element guidance is struggling with, especially in contrast to the incoming warm front which is timed well to support afternoon convection. Mid-level lapse rates of about 6.8 C/km could be better. Aloft, a 45-knot jet could be enough to sustain storms, yet the best upper-level support is well north across the upper Great Lakes. Ensemble high resolution guidance generally suggests a weakening line of convection arrives by mid-afternoon and presents a non-zero chance for damaging wind gusts amid 1500 j/kg of CAPE and about 30 knots of shear.

Given the anticipated modest state of this afternoon`s convection, round two overnight could prove more potent. Starting with the heavy rain concern, multiple forecast models are latching onto a complex of rain and storms that becomes anchored over south- central Lake Michigan extending east through the Indiana-Michigan state line. Storms develop along the warm front amid a strengthening low-level jet. Michiana communities from La Porte and Michigan city, east to the greater Elkhart-Goshen metro areas could be at risk for urban flooding from rain rates of at least 1" per hour (likely more) and rainfall totals in excess of 2-2.5 inches if forecasts verify. Boundary-parallel flow (which is especially weak and convergent at the surface), 500-mb dew point depressions of 1 to 2 C indicate a very deep moisture profile, along with warm cloud depths nearing 14,000 ft. Swift 850-mb flow of about 30-40 knots and generally dry recent conditions may work to reduce the flood risk. Nonetheless, if training storms can develop, heavy rain and localized urban flooding are a concern tonight.

In addition to the flooding threat, any supercells that develop in the vicinity of the warm front pose a risk for all hazards including tornadoes. Lapse Rates once again pose a challenge, as does overall storm evolution, but 0-1km SRH of nearly 300 m2/s2 results in shallow but looping hodographs. The previously mentioned moisture-rich atmosphere may prove challenging for hail, but as storms congeal into a line, damaging wind gusts would become the primary hazard through early Monday morning.

We`ll face another possible round of severe thunderstorms late Monday afternoon and evening as energetic upper-level flow remains in place. This too could pose a heavy rain risk with many of the same ingredients in place. Tuesday will be worth watching for more storms until high pressure finally noes in by Wednesday to steer this active pattern elsewhere. Late-week low pressure brings another chance for storms. High temperatures will be near to slightly below normal through the 7-day period.
