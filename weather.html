.DISCUSSION... Issued at 103 PM EST Thu Nov 26 2026

A large eastern CONUS low pressure system, double-barreled with one center lifting up the east coast and another taking a northeast track just west of the Appalachians, will continue to provide the local area with blustery/cool conditions and a steady rain for the remainder of today through at least Friday morning. Aggressive moisture surge and isentropic ascent within a 50-65 kt easterly jet in the 850-700 mb layer brings more moderate rainfall this afternoon and evening. Deformation forcing lingering on the western flank of the system by later tonight and Friday with rainfall intensity and coverage likely waning a bit with time, especially by later Friday morning and afternoon when some dry pockets emerge. Modest CAA on the backside of the low cold make it possible for wet snow to mix in later tonight into Friday morning in spots. Still not expecting much in the way of impacts given the wet ground and sfc temps/dewpoints holding in the mid-upper 30s. The primary impacts from this system still appear to be the widespread 1" or so rainfall totals, and gusty northeast winds this afternoon-evening in the 30-40 mph range.

Saturday will feature weak warm advection and some sunshine as the large low pressure system exits east and a trailing small scale perturbation bypasses north of the area. A deeper, more progressive shortwave trough remains on target to track east through the OH Valley and Great Lakes Sunday into Sunday evening. Ensemble and deterministic model solutions now generally agree on an associated sfc low track through the OH Valley. Forcing and moisture return will bring another round of primarily rain with amounts up to near a half inch possible. Much of next week then appears mainly dry with gradual moderation thanks to a transition to a lower amplitude, split flow regime.
