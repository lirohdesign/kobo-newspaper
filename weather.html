.DISCUSSION... Issued at 515 AM EDT Sat Jul 18 2026

A shortwave trough moving through the cyclonic flow on the eastern side of high amplitude ridging over the Plains and into central Canada. A cold front associated with this shortwave will begin to push south into our area around midday. SBCAPE values of 2500-3000 J/kg will be available along with bulk shear values of 25 to around 50 kts. Surface dew points in the mid 70s will provide ample moisture and PW values are around the 2 inch mark today. Storms today should have a fairly progressive speed so will help keep the flooding risk a bit lower than the last couple of days when the steering flow was much slower. Helicity values are forecast to be only around 50 m2/s2 which will keep the risk of tornadic activity low. However, with any boundary in close proximity to storms there could be an isolated brief spin up or two. Lapse rates of 6.5-7.0 deg/km will help to allow for some hail production however not ideal for large hail especially with the very humid and warm lower levels but the melting of hail coupled with the heavier downpour potential will make outflow gusts the biggest threat with the storms today. SPC did add a fairly small section of enhanced to our far eastern portions of the CWA in Henry and Putnam counties of Ohio with the better dynamics further east of our area. However, the majority of the area is currently under a Slight risk today.

In the wake of the cold front as it pushes southward a more northerly flow will arrive and this will make for hazardous beach conditions over the southeastern Lake Michigan and wave heights from 3 to 6 feet on the big lake. A Beach hazards statement has been issued to address this threat as well as a Small Craft Advisory. Drier airmass will also accompany the frontal passage.

Sunday will be a bit cooler and drier with a more northwesterly flow aloft and high temperatures will be mainly in the low to mid 80s and dew points only in the 60s. We will also see a short break in precipitation chances on Sunday into Monday before another trough pushes into the lower Great Lakes region on Monday evening. This will also bring another chance of showers and thunderstorms to the area. A few of the storms may become strong to severe so will need to watch this in the coming days.

A northwesterly flow aloft sets up again over the region by Tuesday and remains through the rest of next week and this will help to keep temperatures slightly cooler to near normal values.
