.DISCUSSION... Issued at 330 AM EDT Thu Oct 22 2026

A short wave trough continues to dive southeast across southeast Ontario this morning. Low level ridging is being reinforced behind this short wave across the Mid MS River Valley, and this will prolong the period of weak gradient that has been in place locally the past several days. Ideal radiational cooling conditions this morning will continue to support some patchy fog potential, and present indications still suggest that southeast portions of the area downwind of eastern Great Lakes may experience a brief period of greater fog coverage this morning south of US Route 24 across northeast Indiana and northwest Ohio. Otherwise for today large scale flow amplification is expected as a stronger upper vort lobe across southeast Canada phases with an Atlantic vort max. Meanwhile, a cut-off upper low will continue to migrate slowly eastward into southern California this evening. This evolution will result an amplifying low/upper level ridging across central CONUS and south central Canada. Low level/near sfc winds will veer more easterly as this amplification occurs which will result in continued weak thermal advections over the next 24 hours. Thus, a persistence forecast has been utilized once again today in terms of max temps. The veering low level flow may allow some slightly better low level moisture to drop south into the area from the Great Lakes, but the only possible effect from this weak advection would be some very low coverage diurnal cu that will not have any impact on temps today.

This transition back to low level easterlies will likely support additional stratus/fog development downwind of the eastern Great Lakes tonight, with a potential of some dense fog early Friday morning across northwest Ohio, south central Michigan, and possibly into portions of northeast Indiana. Low temperatures tonight will drop back into the low 40s once again, but may dip into upper 30s for favored, cool low-lying spots.

Guidance is starting to come into a bit better agreement regarding large scale flow through the weekend. A stronger and more progressive northern stream upper level system will shift across south central Canada this weekend which will drive a cold front boundary southward across the Great Lakes. Track of this northern stream wave is expected to remain well to the north, and departure of this wave will likely hang up the front north of the local area through the weekend. With forcing/frontal position to the north, and narrow zone of better pre-frontal moisture transport remaining from the Central Plains to the northern Great Lakes, a dry forecast remains intact through Monday. The warmest days of this forecast period still look to be Sunday/Monday as veered low level flow allows for some modest increase in low level warm advection.

By Tuesday/Wednesday the southern stream cut-off low is expected to eject east and dampen. This wave could interact with the stalled frontal boundary across the Upper MS Valley to the Great Lakes with some weak isentropic ascent possible. Given dampening nature of this wave and likely marginal moisture profiles, blended PoPs remain only in the 10 to 20 percent range toward the end of this forecast valid period.
