.DISCUSSION... Issued at 527 AM EDT Fri Jul 31 2026

One last dry day before chances for showers and storms move into the area for the weekend (50-90 percent chances), and persist on and off through next week (20-40 percent). Expect increasing clouds today with a low chance far west of a few showers towards the evening hours. Expect rain chances to increase and spread eastward through the night, with the best potential west of I 69. More widespread rain showers and scattered storms persist Saturday (70-90 percent chance). Additionally, SPC issued a marginal risk for isolated strong/severe storms Saturday afternoon, mainly including a small portion of our Indiana counties along/south of IN-218/IN-18 (Wells, Grant, Blackford, Jay, and Adams Counties). The primary threat with any stronger storms would be damaging winds up to 60 mph. Otherwise, the main hazards with storms will be gusty winds, lightning, and heavy rain. Localized flooding could occur in areas that see higher rates, depending on how fast the system moves/moisture convergence focuses. The marginal risk is associated with a more narrow axis of sfc instability (300-800 J/kg sfc cape) that reaches into the aforementioned area, with decent moisture transport (Td`s in upper 60s, low 70s) and 0-6 km bulk shear ranging from 30-50 knots (per most guidance, the NAM12 has 50-60kts but closer to NE IN/NW OH...as it is further north than the other guidance). Don`t expect any widespread severe weather given paltry mid level lapse rates at this point.

Temperatures will be in the 80s today, then fall into the 70s, low 80s for Saturday into Monday. Temperatures rise into the low-mid 80s once again Tuesday into Friday.

Our upper level ridge and subsequent surface high will drift eastward today, with a broad upper level trough/closed low dropping into the IA/IL/IN/KY area today into Sunday, where it meanders slowly east (or slightly northeast) from there. There is still a bit of a difference in the placement of the upper trough/vort max amongst guidance. Because of this, the surface low is placed differently, with discrepancies really showing up by 00z Sun. Some of the guidance takes the surface low further south (centered near Indy/KY) whereas the NAM shifts the low more northeast into Lake Erie/Southern Ontario. With the blocking pattern establishing itself through Monday as mentioned in the previous discussion, the trough may linger longer-especially through Sunday, but could be as late as Mon/Tue. Increased pops substantially Sunday to account for this slower movement, with the best potential in the morning along/south of US 24 (55-85 percent). Elsewhere, chances range from 20-50 percent Sunday.

There is a moderate swim risk for Lake Michigan beaches along both La Porte County, IN and Southern Berrien County, MI on Saturday, with easterly winds strengthening and shifting to the northeast at 15 to 25 knots later in the day. Waves will build up to around 3 feet by late afternoon, greatest near New Buffalo, MI to Michigan City, IN.

There is a high swim risk for all beaches in Berrien and La Porte Counties on Sunday. Northeast winds will be around 15 to 25 knots with gusts up to 30 knots, shifting to the north Sunday afternoon. Waves continue to build from 3 to 5 feet Saturday evening to around 4 to 7 feet by Sunday afternoon-with the highest waves from Warren Dunes to Michigan City, IN. Expect a strong southward flowing longshore current and strong structural currents, especially on the north sides of piers and breakwalls. Rip currents are expected. Those planning to visit the beaches should stay out of the water.

Issued a small craft advisory from 18z Saturday into 9z Monday for both marine zones. For now, held off on issuing a beach hazards statement-prefer to wait until the next wave model run this afternoon to narrow down timing/increase confidence given it`s still beyond 24 hours.
