.DISCUSSION... Issued at 301 AM EDT Sun Aug 2 2026

Cancelled the flood watch early for portions of Northern IN as most of the high-res guidance has backed off on the higher rainfall totals in those areas. Otherwise, the deformation zone associated with the upper low/trough (which are presently occurring where we didn`t get as much precipitation earlier today) moves through. A shortwave will drop south across Lake MI/Lower MI around 18-00z before the upper ridge moves in and puts an end to precip chances. Surface winds shift from more northeast to N-NW behind this feature near the lake, with an area of convergence developing. Have pops persisting through the day, with around 20-50 percent northwest and then 60-90 percent along/south of US 24. The environment supports efficient rain-producing cells, so if there are storms that end up redeveloping/training over the same area or there are cells that remain fairly stationary for longer periods of time as the broader surface low/trough meanders E-NE, we could end up with some localized flooding. High-res guidance focuses the best potential in NW OH and portions of Northern Indiana. Kept the watch going for the IN counties, and then expanded the watch into NW Ohio in collaboration with NWS CLE/DTX. Around an inch or so of precipitation has fallen in many areas within the watch, and an additional 1 to 2 inches is expected through the day (of course, locally higher amounts possible).

With the trough sinking down this afternoon, present NNE flow shift more N-NW for a period on Lake Michigan. Waves are presently at around 2-4 feet, lowest up near South Haven, MI where wave periods are about 4 seconds. Further south near Michigan City, waves are greatest with longer periods of around 6 seconds. Yesterday afternoon/evening, waves at Michigan city got up to around 5 feet, and around 4 feet near Warren Dunes, MI. Northern Berrien (including Silver Beach in St. Joseph, MI) and the South Haven area (buoy 45168) lingered around 2 feet. We are in the `lull-period` where waves may briefly drop early this morning into the first part of the day, but expect them to reach heights of 4 to 7 feet as the flow becomes more onshore (vs somewhat shore parallel). Wave periods begin to lengthen to more like 6-8 seconds by late afternoon and early evening, especially further south. Will persist with the current marine headlines where Southern Berrien County, MI and La Porte County, IN are in a beach hazards statement all day today and continuing into Monday morning. Northern Berrien County, MI (including Silver Beach in St. Joseph, MI) will be mostly moderate swim risk conditions until 5 pm EDT, when winds and waves pick up. Have the Beach Hazards Statement starting at that time and persisting through Monday morning. The small craft advisory for the marine zones remains the same as well, with winds/waves dropping below criteria around 5 am EDT.

Expect a strong longshore current moving from N to S along the shore, with strong structural currents expected (particularly on the north sides of piers and breakwalls). Rip currents are expected given increasing wave heights and lengthening wave periods, especially this afternoon when winds/waves become more directly onshore (vs more oblique or shore-parallel as they are this morning). Wave heights diminish late tonight/early Mon AM towards around 2-3 feet, but wave periods remain around 6-7 seconds through early Monday afternoon. Kept the BH.S End time at 8 am Mon, with a moderate swim risk forecast for all but northern Berrien County, MI during the day on Monday. By the afternoon, waves should be only around 1-2 feet.

Mostly dry conditions tonight into Wednesday with an upper ridge/surface high in place. Highs will be in the upper 70s and low to mid 80s, warmest Wednesday. Chances for showers and storms return Wednesday night and persist into the weekend as a broad upper level low moves across the Canadian Prairie and into Hudson Bay. This will bring a trough and cold front into the CWA by Wed night-sinking southeastward and stalling somewhere across our CWA into Thursday. Guidance is all over the place for Friday into the weekend, with some shifting the boundary southeast out of our area and others bringing in a secondary trough axis that keeps potential for precipitation going into the weekend. The best potential for showers/storms (30-60 percent) will be Wednesday night into Thursday, then lower chances (20 to 40 percent) persist into the weekend given model discrepancies. Highs will be in the 80s Thursday into the weekend, warmest Sunday.
