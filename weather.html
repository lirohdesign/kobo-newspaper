.DISCUSSION... Issued at 320 AM EDT Wed Oct 21 2026

Satellite imagery early this morning depicts an expansive area of stratus/fog across northwest Ohio, with widespread dense fog developing across south central Lower Michigan and northwest Ohio. Weak easterly flow associated with eastern portions of low level anticyclone will support westward migration of this stratus/fog into far northeast Indiana over the next several hours. A Dense Fog Advisory has issued earlier this morning for Hillsdale Co MI and all of NW Ohio counties, and based on current trends, a westward expansion will likely be required. With expansive low level anticyclone in place and very weak low level flow, diurnal mixing will likely be slow to erode stratus/fog, with areas of fog persisting through the 14 to 15Z timeframe.

Otherwise for today will be monitoring the progress of a progressive mid/upper level short wave trough across the Upper Midwest this morning as it reaches the western Great Lakes region. A narrow axis of mid level moisture/patchy mid level clouds will likely remain north of the local area while another area of mid clouds associated with mid level short wave progressing through mean upper ridging will largely remain displaced to the west from the Central Plains to the Mid MS Valley. A persistence forecast largely utilized for high temperature forecast today as little thermal advections have been noted over past 24 hours with similar poor mixing profiles today and similar good amounts of insolation.

Passage of the Great Lakes short wave this evening will allow for reinforcement and southwestward shift to low level anticyclone across the region. This should yield a weak northeasterly low level flow profile for tonight which once again raises the question of some enhancement to fog development downwind of eastern Great Lakes. Given northeast trajectories, greatest confidence in fog development tonight is far NE IN/NW OH mainly along and south of US Route 24, but some patchy fog will be possible once again for many areas east of I-69. THe fog may become locally dense once again early Thursday morning.

A stable wave pattern will maintain through the first part of the weekend with an eventual shift of longwave upper ridge axis east of the local area by 2nd half of the weekend. This should allow for Sunday to realize the warmest temperatures of the period in the low to mid 70s. Longwave predictability begins to break down early next week with large discrepancies in how phasing is depicted with western CONUS closed upper height perturbations among various deterministic/ensemble guidance. Some indications that stronger frontal forcing will develop northwest of the local area late weekend/early next week as southwest CONUS upper wave pushes east and dampens, but given uncertainty in progression and depth of this wave as it reaches central CONUS argues for maintenance of mainly dry forecast at this time with expectation that frontal forcing will wane through time.
