.DISCUSSION... Issued at 214 AM EDT Sun Oct 18 2026

Surface high pressure slowly slides eastward today from the southern Great Lakes and then a couple more surface high pressure systems pass by to the north of the Great Lakes Tuesday and Thursday. Meanwhile, mid level ridging continues to keep the area within a large scale area of subsidence quelling any large scale area of rain through this week and probably through next weekend. This is even as a cold front stalls around the area Monday night/Tuesday and another cold front moves through later Wednesday/Thursday AM time frame. These FROPAs will be dry FROPAs. Troughs attempt to make their way eastward but continue to run into the stout mid level ridge east of the Rockies through this work week. As such, various vort maxes/surface lows take a northeast trek from the Plains into the western Great Lakes/southcentral Canada area. Attempts by WAA to affect the area cause a slow warming trend through the 60s today and Monday, reaching the 70s for highs on Tuesday southwest of US-30 through this week. Daily lows look to trend warmer as well beyond this morning. There`s a marginal chance for frost Monday morning with about 3 hours of 38 degree temps or less, especially south of US-24. However, another frost/freeze issuance will depend on if the growing season continues or if this morning`s temps ended things. The area is fairly dry with MinRH values dipping into the 20s and 30s of percents today through Tuesday, but low level lapse rates aren`t as strong as they could be, and even more of a factor, wind gusts are below considerable thresholds. Future dry days are possible beyond Tuesday, but wind gusts don`t look respectable at this time. It is also possible our forecast dew points are too high as currently constituted beyond Tuesday.
