.DISCUSSION... Issued at 620 AM EDT Sat Aug 1 2026

The occluding surface low responsible for our active weather this weekend is centered over southeastern IA/NW IL as of this writing, with a broad shield of rain showers and isolated storms extending from up near Milwaukee southeastward into Indy and KY/TN. A 500mb trough encompasses the Great Lakes region, with the closed low/associated speed max near the four corners. Ongoing steady rain showers or drizzle extends from from Michigan City, IN into Marion, IN and is lifting northeast with time. Beyond that, light east- northeast flow persists across the forecast area, with some light shower activity in Lower MI and near Lima, OH.

As the trough aloft/closed low digs southward into southern IN/KY through Sunday morning, the surface low drifts eastward, centering around IND by 2 am EDT tonight (models still don`t quite have the exact location pinned down). Shower activity will become more widespread through the afternoon into tonight as the better upper level forcing arrives and several waves of precip rotating around the surface low to our S-SE move through. Much of the guidance has a persistent/recurrent rainfall along and south of US 30 today into tonight as the low pivots, and then by Sunday a robust deformation zone extending from the low near Indy (depends on the model) up into southeast Lower MI. The NAM really latches onto this, and it appears to be the furthest north of the solutions (centers low from Indy to near Logansport, IN). This keeps the axis from Logansport into Hillsdale through much of Sunday. There is still lower confidence as to whether this feature develops as robustly as indicated, and if it sinks southeastward with time (thus moving the precip axis and distributing the rain) or stays in generally the same location through Sun. If it stays stationary like the NAM depicts, some areas that see repeated thunderstorms or heavy rain could end up with 4 inches of rain or more. After a review of the latest guidance with the neighboring offices, decided not to issue a flood watch given there are still questions as to where the band/earlier showers and storms set up and if they move or stay relatively stationary into Sunday.

There is a marginal risk for isolated strong to severe storms this afternoon and evening in Indiana and Ohio, generally south of US 30. The primary threat with any stronger storms would be damaging wind gusts to 60 mph. Models suggest around 500-1000 J/kg of surface CAPE this afternoon, with 0-6 km bulk shear around 25-40 knots along/south of US 30, which is plenty to work with in terms of stronger storm potential.

Otherwise, low chance pops (20-40 percent) through the work week, with highs in the 70s, low-mid 80s.

High swim risk and beach hazards statement for La Porte County, IN and Southern Berrien County, MI from late this afternoon into Monday morning. Winds strengthen out of the northeast at 15 to 25 knots and become more northerly through Sunday night. Waves of 1-2 feet today will build to around 3-5 feet by late this afternoon and evening (highest near New Buffalo, MI into Michigan City, IN). On Sunday, waves will build to 4 to 7 feet by the late afternoon.

Northern Berrien County, MI (including Silver Beach) will see lower wave heights today, generally 1 to 2 feet building to around 3 feet by late this afternoon/evening. Waves linger in the 2-3 ft range until late Sunday afternoon, where they build to 3 to 5 feet. Have a moderate swim risk for today (into early Sunday), and then a high swim risk for Sunday afternoon into Monday morning. The Beach Hazards Statement for this county is in effect starting late Sunday afternoon.

Expect a strong southward flowing longshore current to develop with the north-northeast flow later today into tonight and persist through Sunday, in addition to strong structural currents (particularly on the north sides of piers and breakwalls). Rip currents are expected, especially where wave heights are greatest and flow is more northerly. If visiting the beaches this weekend, monitor the beach forecast.
