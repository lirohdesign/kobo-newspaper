.DISCUSSION... Issued at 320 AM EDT Sat Aug 29 2026

Overall no major changes in forecast thinking from the past several cycles. Low chances of rain showers for northern locations are expected tonight through Sunday. A warming trend still remains of high confidence this weekend into early next week. Heat indices from the mid 90s to 100 are possible Tuesday and Wednesday afternoons.

An expansive low level anticyclone persists this morning from western New York into southern Missouri. Little movement in this anticyclone is expected through tonight as local area remains downstream of mid/upper level ridge axis across the Central and Northern Plains.

A strong low level jet in vicinity of the upper level ridge axis across the Northern Plains has led to development of convection early this morning across eastern South Dakota. This convection should augment a smaller scale short wave progressing through this ridge axis, and upper level wave pattern remains supportive of this short wave dropping east-southeast to the southern Great Lakes later this afternoon/early evening. A pocket of enhanced mid level westerlies associated with this convectively enhanced short wave should shift across the southern Great Lakes region tonight with a period of sharp mid level moisture advection. Low level moisture advection will be a bit more limited given what should remain some unfavorable low level flow trajectories arising from the persistence of the low level anticyclone. This evolution should allow for some very weak elevated instability to shift eastward across southern/central Lower Michigan this evening. Passage of this short wave, broad isentropic upglide, and weak elevated instability arising from elevated moisture transport could be enough for a few showers across the north tonight.

Stronger southwesterly low level flow from the Plains will migrate eastward to the Mid MS Valley/southern Great Lakes on Sunday with some increase in isentropic lift. Difficult to discern much else in terms of more pronounced mid/upper level forcing by this time, but overall pattern with advective forcing seems to support at least some slight chance PoPs across the northern half of the area. An isolated thunderstorm is possible, but better low level moisture transport and more pronounced MUCAPEs should reside back across the Corn Belt. It will be noticeably warmer on Sunday with high temperatures into the middle 80s. With better moisture transport still displaced to the west, sfc dew points should be limited to the mid 60s locally.

For Sunday night into Monday, some subtle positive height tendencies are expected across the western Great Lakes downstream of more substantial upper trough tracking across southern Saskatchewan/Manitoba. Eastward progression of the upper ridge axis and this upper trough riding this ridge should result in some veering of low level flow late Monday and eastward advection of richer low level moisture across Corn Belt/Mid MS Valley. This period will also mark the beginning of a stronger mid level thermal ridge building across the area. A few showers and storms are possible as this mid level thermal ridge builds in later Monday night but confidence in precip is low. The main weather story for Tuesday/Wednesday will focus on building heat and humidity with a potential of heat indices from the mid 90s to around 100 each afternoon.

On Wednesday, the aforementioned upper trough will be tracking across the western Great Lakes and eventually eastern Great Lakes Wednesday evening. An associated frontal boundary may be focal point for isolated-scattered showers and storms somewhere across Great Lakes region, but questions remain as to instability magnitudes given presence of warm mid level thermal profiles and good possibility that sfc/near sfc convection is suppressed. May need to watch for any southward migrating outflows from Great Lakes convection aiding in convective development later Wednesday.

A period of cold advection is expected Wed night/Thu as this front drops into the region. Medium range guidance deterministic solutions differ sharply by next Thu/Fri, but overall ensemble mean trend is for some subtle amplification of longwave western CONUS trough-central CONUS ridge-eastern CONUS trough pattern. Multi-ensesmble mean position of upper ridge axis is west of the local area which would keep greater heat concerns west of the local area Thursday through Saturday. However, occasional short waves in northwest flow aloft and potential episodes of positive low-mid level theta-e advection accompanying these waves may provide some additional chances of rain in the days 5 to 7 period.
