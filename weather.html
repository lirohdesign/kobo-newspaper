.DISCUSSION... Issued at 1234 PM EST Wed Nov 18 2026

A large upper level ridge continues to amplify into the Midwest and Great Lakes regions with the dry and very mild weather pattern expected to last through this week. Surface high pressure will prevail with litle movement from where it is anchored over the Appalachian mountains. Southerly flow and increased WAA will funnel in much warmer air from the Gulf and southern US. There are no hazardous weather concerns this week as both high and low temperatures will continue to climb well above seasonal averages. High temperatures will soar into the low to mid 60s starting tomorrow, which will be 15 to 20 degrees above normal for this time of the year! Lows will be above freezing in the 40s. Friday and Saturday will be the warmest days of the week, with near record high temperatures.

As the ridge breaks down by late in the week, rain chances return on Sunday as a cold front moves through. Compared to yesterday, models have slowed down the timing of this front into our area by about 12 hours; wheras before it looked like we could get some scattered showers Saturday night, now it appears like Saturday will stay dry and Sunday now has the bulk of the rain chances. By the time it makes its way into our forecast area, the cold front should be fairly weak. With a limited amount of instability and potentially unfavorable morning timing to work with, only light rainfall amounts of 0.10-0.25" are anticipated on Sunday. Have maintained low to medium PoPs on Sunday with 20-40% chances. Following the frontal passage, temperatures will moderate back towards seasonable levels with highs in the 40s. There are no real signs of winter yet as the mild weather pattern looks to hold at least through the end of meteorological fall.
