.DISCUSSION... Issued at 233 AM EDT Tue Jul 28 2026

A surface trough is pivoting through our area currently, sparking a few widely scattered storms across northern Indiana and northwest Ohio. Any stronger storms may produce small hail and gusty winds, although they are expected to stay below severe limits. This activity is expected to push off to the southeast later this morning, bringing precipitation chances to an end. A trailing cold front is currently positioned from Milwaukee, WI to Muskegon, MI to Alpena, MI and is moving slowly south. This cold front will move through our area later this morning from 12-18Z. Before the front gets here, I would not be surprised if patchy dense fog attempts to develop from 09-13Z this morning. Surface observations do not show any fog yet but we will continue to monitor through the overnight hours. Surface conditions are decently favorable for fog with the saturated ground from yesterday`s afternoon and evening storms. Winds are still a bit elevated at 5 to 10 mph out of the south and will eventually switch to be coming from the west closer to daybreak.

Dry air, low humidity, sunshine, and cooler temperatures are expected behind the front. Behind the frontal passage, winds will switch again to be coming from the north; a strong CAA push will allow for high temperatures to be below normal today only topping out in the upper 70s to low 80s. Locations closer to Lake Michigan in northwest Indiana and southwest Lower Michigan may not even get out of the mid 70s today. The HRRR smoke model depicts wildfire smoke moving in aloft today from active wildfires burning in the Pacific Northwest. Have adjusted sky cover up to 35% through Wednesday morning to account for hazy skies. No impacts to surface air quality or visibilities are expected.

Several more seasonable and dry days are expected Wednesday and Tuesday as high pressure builds into the Great Lakes region midweek. Expect highs to gradually warm by a few degrees each day through Friday as the airmass moderates. It will be dry at least through Thursday; an incoming frontal system over the central Plains will move into the mid Mississippi River valley by Saturday, with intermittent low chances (20-30%) for rain/storms returning to our forecast area by late Friday and into the weekend.

Although it may look like a great day to head out to Lake Michigan beaches today, as winds shift to become northerly, dangerous swimming conditions are expected to develop for southeast Lake Michigan beaches on Tuesday. Buoys already show waves of 2 to 3 feet offshore of La Porte County, IN and Berrien County, MI. Waves will reach 3 to 5 feet Tuesday morning and will continue to build to 5 to 8 feet by this afternoon and evening following the cold frontal passage. With direct onshore flow, strong structural, rip, and longshore currents are expected. Structural currents will be especially strong on the north side of piers. Stay out of the water and stay off of the piers! Beach Hazards Statements are in effect from now through daybreak Wednesday for La Porte County, IN and Berrien County, MI. A moderate swim risk is ahead for Wednesday with waves of 2 to 3 feet, especially in the morning. Waves are anticipated to diminish during the day and a welcome return to much calmer conditions is ahead for Thursday.
