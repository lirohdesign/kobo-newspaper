.DISCUSSION... Issued at 155 AM EDT Sat Jul 25 2026

Winds will be out of the southwest today as our area is on the backside of an expansive high pressure system centered over the Northeastern US. As WAA ramps up throughout the day, high temperatures will be in the upper 70s to low 80s with a noticeable increase in humidity compared to the past few days as dew points hover around 60 to 65 degrees by the afternoon. Mid level stratus clouds are expected to build in later today. Also, skies may appear hazy at times as smoke from ongoing wildfires in the Pacific Northwest drifts in but remains elevated. No impacts to air quality or visibility are expected. In coordination with neighboring offices to our west, have trended temperatures and dew points down a degree or two due to increased confidence in partly to mostly cloudy skies during peak diurnal heating.

Hot and humid conditions on Sunday and Monday with potential for severe weather. West/southwest winds will advect in higher dewpoints around 70 to 75 degrees by Sunday afternoon and again on Monday. Locally higher dewpoints will be possible due to increased evapotranspiration from crops. Confidence continues to increase in the `ring of fire` pattern setting up where our forecast area will be on the periphery of a building upper level ridge centered over the Central Plains. With northwest steering flow aloft, one or more MCSs could develop and impact our forecast area late Sunday through early Tuesday. The `ring of fire` pattern often has a high ceiling for repeated rounds of severe weather but it is also highly conditional. There is a wide spread of solutions within model guidance which lends itself to considerable uncertainty still. The hot and humid conditions will fuel a potentially volatile environment for storms with up to 3000-4000 J/kg SBCAPE and 40 kts of shear. Convective initiation remains uncertain as to exactly when and where it will occur Sunday evening; the daytime hours will be mainly dry, with the chances for strong to severe storms anticipated to increase after 8 PM EDT lasting through about 4 AM EDT Monday. The greatest uncertainties at this time are the potential for capping and what the forcing mechanism will be for convective initiation. As mentioned in the previous discussion, determining if the catalyst for storms will be upper level vorticity maxima, the upper level jet, or remnant outflow boundaries/MCVs will be vital in understanding when and where storms will track. Given this uncertainty, a broad Marginal Risk (level 1 of 5) for severe weather encompasses our forecast area Sunday night. what happens late Sunday will influence what occurs on Monday; have maintained the best chances for rain/storms on Monday in the evening and early overnight hours, although this could change. Storms are expected to grow upscale into a squall line or mature MCS, with damaging winds as the main threat. A brief embedded tornado or two will be possible as well as 0-1 SRH values approach 150-200 m2/s2 Sunday night and again on Monday.

Exactly how hot and humid it will get on Monday will be determined by storms Sunday night. Have maintained highs in the upper 80s to low 90s for now, but diurnal heating may be disrupted or stunted by lingering clouds on Monday depending on what happens Sunday. Monday`s heat indices are around 100 degrees in the forecast, but will hold off on heat headlines for now until better confidence arises in what will happen Sunday night.

Tuesday and beyond will see a reprieve in the active weather pattern as high pressure builds over the Great Lakes. Dry and seasonable with highs in the mid 80s midweek. As the upper level ridge builds and expands eastward, hot and humid conditions may return by the end of next week.
