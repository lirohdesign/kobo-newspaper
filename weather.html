.DISCUSSION... Issued at 101 PM EST Thu Nov 19 2026

Surface high pressure centered over the East Coast continues to provide tranquil, dry weather through Saturday. With increasing WAA and dry air in the low levels, temperatures will remain in the mid 60s for highs tomorrow and Saturday. Highs should be within 5 degrees of the daily record on Friday and Saturday at Fort Wayne and South Bend!

As high pressure gets pushed eastward Saturday due to influence from a trough over the Four Corners region, rain chances return on Sunday as a cold front approaches. Chances for rain currently stand at 60- 80%, however, the day will not be a washout. Scattered, light showers will move through the area at times on Sunday during the day. The bulk of the rain looks to move through before the front does; rainfall amounts are expected to be light as showers should outrun the better low level moisture and lift with the actual front. It will be breezy on Sunday with a tightening pressure gradient in the vicinity of the front; Wind gusts will be between 20-25 mph with a shift in direction from southwest to northwest.

Following the frontal passage, CAA and northerly flow ensues with temperatures moderating back towards seasonable levels with highs in the upper 30s to low 40s. The GFS and ECMWF are in solid agreement next week of 850 mb temps as low as -6C to -10C Monday into Tuesday. As a high pressure system spills into the Great Lakes region from central Canada, the forecast is leaning dry for early into middle part of next week. An early preview of Thanksgiving shows dry conditions with highs around 40s. Given that a strong El Nino is ongoing, a more active pattern may develop across the southeastern US with the jet stream keeping activity suppressed to the south. There is some indication in the long range guidance of a `Miller A` type system developing mid to late next week, but a lot of uncertainty remains. It is plausible we get a glancing blow of some rain/snow showers towards the end of the month, but there are no real signs of winter yet.
