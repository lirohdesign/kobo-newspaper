.DISCUSSION... Issued at 210 PM EST Mon Nov 9 2026

Mild weather will continue through Tuesday with slightly above normal temperatures and dry conditions due to a large surface high pushing up from the south. Mostly clear skies tonight will allow temperatures to fall into the lower 40s. It will not be quite as cold as last night since southwest winds will contribute to better boundary layer mixing. This should also keep fog and frost from forming. Winds will diminish considerable though by Tuesday night to near calm, and radiational cooling will drop temperatures into the mid 30s. The HRRR is also indicating a large area of dense fog developing across the region on Wednesday morning, which may require advisories.

A deep trough then moves across the Midwest on Wednesday, bringing rain to the local area by late afternoon and evening. The accompanying cold front will also bring breezy north winds going into Thursday. This northerly flow will result in lake effect rain showers, and then possibly snow showers on Thursday night and Friday. Right now it is too early to tell exactly how much snow will fall, but with the recent warm weather, roadways should stay warm enough for little to no accumulation. While it will remain cool on Friday, temps will rise into the low 40s, causing subsequent melting. Showers should taper off on Saturday as the large scale low advects farther east of the region and high pressure builds back in from the west. Dry weather is then expected on Sunday and Monday with gradually warming temperatures. Mondays highs are forecast to be back in the mid 50s again.
