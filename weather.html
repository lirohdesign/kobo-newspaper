.DISCUSSION... Issued at 325 AM EDT Wed Apr 8 2026

A weak mid level disturbance across the Mid MS River Valley will track across the western Great Lakes this morning. Stronger advective forcing is already underway across northern Illinois where a strong low level theta-e gradient will shift eastward across the southern Great Lakes this morning. Stronger moisture transport in this setup should still be limited to the western Great Lakes, largely bypassing the local area just to the northwest. However, a brief period of light precip is possible as the leading edge of this advective forcing tracks across NW Indiana/SW Lower Michigan. With temperatures looking to be just above freezing across these locations and a very dry initial subcloud layer, precip types should favor flurries/light snow or brief light sleet. Given this dry subcloud layer, the current expectation is for trace precip amounts if precip does occur. By mid-late morning, stronger forcing mechanisms should bypass the local area to the north. Warm advection today will result in temperatures nosing back above normal with coolest temps lingering across south central Lower Michigan which will be slightly divorced from stronger low level warm advection. These warmer sfc temperatures and strengthening low level height gradient should support better mixing today and some 20 to 30 mph gusts midday into this afternoon.

Attention tonight shifts to evolution of a broad upper level low tracking across northern tier of CONUS and a trailing cold frontal boundary. Strongest synoptic forcing with this feature still looks to migrate from northern IA/southern MN early this evening to the western Great Lakes overnight before shifting across southeast Ontario Thursday morning. Some pre-frontal forcing and narrow axis of low level moisture convergence accompanying a strong low level jet downstream of this feature should promote a narrow band of rain dropping across the region late tonight into Thursday AM. Through time Thursday morning, precip coverage should likely diminish as stronger synoptic support shifts off to the southeast Canada leaving this frontal boundary stalled across the area. Precip amounts (around tenth of an inch) late tonight into Thursday should not be of much consequence to water levels on area rivers. Temperatures will continue above normal on Thursday, from mid 60s across Lower Michigan to the low 70s far NE IN/NW OH.

The stalled boundary is likely to become active once again Thursday night into Friday as another mid level wave drops southeast into the area through the downstream portion of the northern stream upper ridge across western CONUS. A ramping up of low level flow/moisture advection should allow for increase in showers and embedded storms accompanying northward retreating boundary Thursday night. Eventually, a cold frontal feature will shift back across the local area with likelihood of showers and embedded storms into Friday. Precip amounts in this round of precip should generally be one quarter of an inch or less and generally not a huge impact on area river trends. May need to watch for axis of heavier amounts of 0.50" or greater across Kankakee/St. Joseph basins from NE IL into SW Lower Michigan late Thursday night/early Friday when sfc boundary is more stationary and where low level jet may interact with this frontal zone for a time.

The low level baroclinic zone should get pushed south of the local area later Friday with primarily quiet conditions for at least first part of the weekend. Northward surging warm front for weekend combined with additional series of smaller scale embedded waves ejecting northeast in southwest upper flow will provide low chances of showers and storms for portions of the area Sunday.

By early next week, the main forecast focus shifts to the evolution of larger scale southern stream cut-off trough and its northeast ejection into central CONUS for Tuesday. This will support two warm days Sunday and Monday with highs back into the 75 to 88 degree range. The ejection of this trough should also draw some better mid level lapse rates northeast from the southern/central Plains once again and the anomalous moisture combined with these steeper lapse rates aloft should be more supportive of increased shower/storm chances early next week. Timing of mid/upper level forcing mechanisms remains an item of lower confidence so an extended period of high chance to likely PoPs had to be maintained early next week. Some strong/severe risk is possible for one of these periods early next week given above factors, but uncertainty in synoptic boundary placement and timing of these periods of stronger synoptic forcing still lead to low confidence in this regard. Confidence remains on the higher side in much above normal temps Sun-Tue likely trending back to near normal or slightly below normal toward the middle of next week.
