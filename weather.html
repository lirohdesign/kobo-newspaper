.DISCUSSION... Issued at 335 AM EDT Tue Sep 1 2026

Showers have largely diminished across the area early this morning. Regional radar mosaic does depict some low coverage showers across portions of north central Illinois in what has been a persistent zone of westerly flow isentropic upglide from north central Illinois into northwest Ohio. One characteristic that has changed over past 6 to 12 hours is the northeast advection of warmer mid level profiles as evident with substantial low level CIN noted per KDVN and KILX 00Z RAOB from last evening. This west-east oriented zone of isentropic forcing will persist through daybreak although in terms of mid/upper level forcing, this does appear to coincide with a lull. Did maintain some 15-20 PoPs across the northern third into mid morning, but weak mid/upper level forcing and somewhat more unfavorable mid level thermal profiles should keep coverage very low if any showers are able to develop.

For this afternoon, some sheared vorticity associated with stronger northern latitude westerlies will top the upper ridge and begin to shift east across the western/southern Great Lakes late this afternoon into early this evening. Primary surface boundary will be displaced well to the northwest but strong buoyancy is expected to develop in pre-frontal environment from northeast IL eastward into southern Lower MI/far northern IN (MLCAPEs around 3000 J/kg possible). The more robust shear profiles should be displaced closer to main sfc boundary across, but deep layer shear magnitudes on the order of 25 to 35 knots will likely have a narrow spatial overlay with this stronger buoyancy north of the US Route 30 corridor. The very warm mid level profiles in place raise some question as to the potential of surface based CIN lingering in the afternoon, although with the very warm/moist near sfc airmass in place, would suspect this CIN will be minimal during peak hating. What appears to be a large component missing today from an appreciable severe threat is any pronounced stronger pre-frontal convergence or trigger mechanism. Aforementioned shear vorticity max will likely be migrating northward across east central WI/central Lower MI later this afternoon/evening, with not a great juxtaposition of forcing and better shear/instability. A Marginal Risk of severe weather has been maintained today for areas roughly north of US Route 30 corridor which seems reasonable given shear/instability combination. Some persistent isentropic upglide in 310-315k layer persists today which could aid in initiation, but overall weak nature to mid/upper level forcing continues to suggest very low confidence in severe weather occurrence today.

For most, the main weather concern today will be high heat and humidity, with warm/moist airmass now firmly in place across the region. Given aforementioned warmer mid level profiles and lack of clear upstream wave early in the day, better insolation should be realized today in comparison to past few days with most areas reaching around 90 or lower 90s for highs. Dew points should be fairly uniform in low to possibly mid 70s across the area which will produce heat indices in the upper 90s to around 105. Given continued low confidence in coverage of showers/storms even across the north today, derived heat indices from this morning`s forecast grids show rather minimal differences across the area with peak afternoon heat indices. Given Wednesday should feature even greater confidence in reaching advisory criteria area-wide, did go ahead and extend the pre-existing Heat Advisory across the remainder of southern Lower Michigan in effect from 18Z today to 00Z Wednesday evening for all areas. Have not expanded this advisory into Thursday yet, but the possibility exists that areas roughly along and south of US 30 may need the Heat Advisory extended into early Thursday evening.

In addition to the ongoing heat concerns for Wednesday, convective potential will once again need to be watched for afternoon/evening. The larger scale primary upper trough will be tracking across western Quebec Wed afternoon but another short wave will be exiting out of the Dakotas with a secondary northward surge of a low level theta-e ridge across Upper Midwest/Corn Belt. Synoptic pattern would tend to favor frontal boundary position north of the local area into early Wednesday evening, but if a more organized area of storms can form across Upper Midwest/Corn Belt Wednesday at nose of this secondary theta-e max, it is possible associated outflow could modulate position of this boundary to the southern Lower Michigan by evening. In any event, stronger mid/upper level forcing still looks to bypass the area to the north, and isolated severe threat may require this southward modulation of effective frontal boundary by upstream outflow which is of course of low confidence at this time. Day 2 Marginal Risk still remains in place given potential of moderate to strong buoyancy and persistence of sufficient deep vertical shear profiles. Chances of storms will likely persist through later night period Wednesday night as some increase in low level southwesterly flow is expected later in the night which could generate some more convection near this southern Great Lakes boundary.

For Thursday, low level front will likely remained stalled across the Great Lakes region given some mid level height recovery across Mid MS Valley/western Great Lakes Thu afternoon. Additional smaller scale short waves progressing through upper ridge could interact with this boundary for additional thunderstorms Thursday afternoon and evening, and perhaps another day of isolated strong/severe potential.

Medium range guidance remains consistent with transition in longwave pattern to more amplitude Fri-Sat with maintenance of central CONUS longwave ridge. This could keep local area positioned along instability gradient for good portion of the late work week/weekend period with some additional shower/storm chances as additional NW waves dive down the ridge. This ridge positioning should end concern for heat headlines later this week with temps likely settling back near normal toward the end of this forecast valid period.
