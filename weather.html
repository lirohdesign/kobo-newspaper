.DISCUSSION... Issued at 109 PM EST Fri Nov 27 2026

An expansive cutoff low was climbing northeastward toward the Northeast today as another trough was moving eastward from the Western US. Warming is expected to take place this afternoon as the main area of vorticity surrounding the upper low departs the area meaning the mix of snow (with times of drizzle because of DGZ saturation) with rain should just become rain this afternoon. At the base of this approaching mid level trough, a moderate shortwave was moving east-northeastward toward the area, but subsidence behind the departing upper low should keep the area mostly dry overnight. There is some indication in the RRFS, the ECMWF, and to some extent, the HRRR that some weak areas of weak precip may attempt to occur Saturday morning mainly north of US-6, but it`ll need to overcome some low level dry air. Temps appear fairly marginal for snow so will lean rain at this juncture. Otherwise, the bulk of the area leans dry Saturday. There could be some 20 mph wind gusts to finish out the day Saturday as the upper low departs the area and weak mixing ensues. Another vort max rounds the base of the trough Saturday through the Southern Plains and approaches the area Sunday morning. The general theme for much of these events where cold air has attempted to wrap in on the back side thus far this winter has been for the cold air to be late to arrive. As 850 mb temperatures approach -6C on the backside of the precip shield, the tendency for the precip shield later Sunday will be to dry out. It`ll be a race against time though that maybe there could be a finish with snow on its back end after sunset Sunday. As cold air continues to push in and the column dries out, an area of increased gradient keeps winds excited overnight and this will allow for the potential for gusts between 25 and 35 mph.

As mentioned above, strong drying within subsidence occurs on the backside of the departing trough as high pressure to our south make its presence felt and mid level heights rise. Low level temperatures also warm back above freezing. It`s interesting to see the GFS lag behind the other models including its AI counterpart for the mid week trough approaching from the southwest. There`s still enough differences in these models to indicate that certainty is low on whether we get rain to end the end of next week. Between the NBM and a blend of the long range ensembles and operational guidance, there seems to be some agreement that the Thursday night into Friday morning time period presents the best chance to see rain. The GFS still lags this and sees the best chance waiting until Friday night/Saturday. It`ll also be a breezy time period with 25 to 35 mph gusts possible.
