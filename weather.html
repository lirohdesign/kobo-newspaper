.DISCUSSION... Issued at 1200 PM EST Wed Nov 11 2026

A vigorous shortwave continues moving southeast from IA this morning through Central IL today, reaching central IN this afternoon. The eventual placement of the warm front will be key in understanding severity, but there appears to be some southward trend in that placement in models between yesterday and today. Key to thunderstorm development appears to be the mid level lapse rates. Hail was forming across eastern IA this morning right on the northern gradient from high to low within an elevated area of instability. This would be the cause of hail should it form across the area today. Such an area of mid level lapse rates moves into areas south of US-30 midday into the afternoon before vacating by 20z. This area is well out in front of the forcing and would likely not be where storms form. The other area into our southwest 1 to 2 tiers of counties has mid level lapse rates from 6.5 to 7C. This overlaps with an area of very weak MUCAPE right around 100 to 250 J/kg in the 20 to 00z window. There is some low level turning to work with within the lowest 1 to 2km of the atmosphere, but given the weak instability and what appears to be an elevated instability nature to the atmospheric column, the tornado threat appears to be low unless we can realize a taller surface instability column. Soundings appear to show a sort of an onion-shaped or shallow inverted-V profile that may be conducive to bringing winds down to the surface alluding to the gusty to damaging wind possibility.

Hot on the heels of this wave is an area of vorticity traveling southward from Canada. As this area travels southward, it deepens and interacts with the departing low pressure area. What`s interesting is the deeper cold is delayed and farther west of Lk MI on Thursday. The ECMWF almost has a drainage signal where the eastern Lk MI area is cold, but over the lake is warmer, even at 850 mb. So, while the QPF signal appears lake effect/lake enhanced, we haven`t gotten the cold air enough to change it over to snow yet. A couple of compact waves traverse southward nearby Lk MI and the RRFS seems to show a trough kicking off a more sustained band Thursday evening. By the time we get to early Fri AM, enough cold air has wrapped in that soundings are beginning to get snow mixed in or changed over. -5 to -6C at 850 mb is right over the southern tear drop of Lk MI on the ECMWF so that makes sense. As various waves/instability troughs traverse southward, finding the exact trajectory will be difficult because there will be some east-west wavering. A slight eastward change can mean the relatively vigorous lift going on from the relatively warm lake and cold airmass interaction can mean quick snow rates getting into LaPorte and maybe Starke counties. These could be rates that overcome the warm ground from how anomalously warm we`ve been this late fall. As such, will continue the mention of the 1 to 2 inches of snow through Friday that could result in LaPorte county especially with that wavering. Model guidance with variable snow ratios continues to back this up. It`ll be interesting to see what happens with snow and if it`ll stay mainly on grassy surfaces or if it`ll have enough oomph to accumulate on roads. Some guidance say it`ll struggle to accumulate on roads Friday morning. The snow band may push eastward Saturday morning toward US-30, but it would appear to be more diffuse at that point and in its dying trend. It`ll also trend back to rain or drizzle as the column warms. Lake clouds could continue through Sunday with the northwest trajectories continuing. It is interesting to see the highs are still forecast to warm into the 40s on Friday and Saturday. That would cut into the accumulation ceilings and I`m curious if we`d have refreezing overnight with sub-freezing lows. This would depend on having standing water left on roads that, with light to calm wind, would indicate it`s possible.

Finally, by the time we get to Monday, trajectories change even as the last vigorous wave traverses the lake Saturday. Surface high pressure is already working to dry low level moisture out this weekend. Highs trend warmer through the 40s to reach the 50s by the middle of next week as the surface high slowly translates eastward. There is a vigorous wave south of the high that moves into the Gulf States, but it`s far enough south to avoid our area. The warm advection pattern returns midweek next week as a wave shifts northeastward from the Central Plains into south-central Canada. As a result, the work week looks dry Monday through at least Thursday. However, lows won`t rise above freezing until Thursday morning.
