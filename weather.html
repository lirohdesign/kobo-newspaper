.DISCUSSION... Issued at 129 AM EDT Tue Aug 11 2026

Quasi-stationary boundary remains in nearly the same area as 24 hours ago, from near Davenport, IA east to Chicago and then curving towards Detroit. Much like last night, an area of showers and storms has exited the forecast area with attention turning upstream across E IA into N IL where CAMs develop additional convection over the next several hours and track E or SE. As of 5Z a few cells have popped from near Gary NW to Poplar Grove IL. Intensification has been on the slower side, but LLJ is starting to increase with 25 to 30 kts now noted on VADs in Iowa and central IL and mesoanalysis shows a pocket of over 2000 J/KG of MLCAPE. Pops have been adjusted somewhat, but may need further changes later depending on evolution. A threat for large hail, locally gusty winds, frequent lightning and heavy rain would be the main concerns.

Towards 12Z most of the activity looks to push away with several CAMs hinting at new development along the washed out boundary from W of Chicago to White county. As the low level flow increases from the SW, this looks to shift NE allowing a small window for rapid destabilization. With MLCAPE over 2000 J/KG, DCAPE over 1000 J/KG and at least modest shear. A line of showers and storms is expected to develop upstream and dive quickly SE, arriving as early as 18Z in W/NW areas. Some concerns that the morning convection could impact the overall strength of the line and possibly focus the greater wind threat SW of us. Upper level flow, approaching wave and other factors above all point towards the potential of a higher end wind event along with a small chance of brief spin ups. New SWODY1 maintains the Enhanced risk across the SW half of the area with a CIG1 hatching for the increased wind threat. Alignment of best pops may change this morning as we see what, if any impacts occur from overnight and early morning convection.

In addition to the severe threat, flooding concerns will remain on the front burner as swaths of heavier rainfall have occurred across the CWA over the past couple of days, most recently in SE area. Given the lower confidence on the evolution of convection through early evening was going to hold on any flood watch, but after collaboration in house and surrounding offices decided to hoist a Flood Watch for all of our IN and OH counties with most of our MI counties spared of the worst rainfall.

The line should quickly push out near/after 00Z Wed with the frontal boundary finally getting a nudge south into central IN. Does this mean that Wed and beyond will see much calmer conditions...probably not as the entire upper level flow really doesn`t change much the remainder of the week and troughs moving through the area will cause the front to shift north and south. As a result, pops remain riddled in several period through the remainder of the forecast. Given the challenges the next 12 to 24 hours, anything outside tonight was left alone.
