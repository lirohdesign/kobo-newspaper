.DISCUSSION... Issued at 225 PM EST Mon Nov 2 2026

Broad advective forcing continues across the region with water vapor imagery and near term hi-res guidance initializations depicting next weak short wave across the Mid MS Valley. THis should allow for a few more waves of drizzle and possibly a few light rain showers this evening but most locations will be dry or experience just trace amounts. As mentioned in previous discussion, guidance has struggled today with temps with a maintenance in blended guidance of a warm bias. Extensive low clouds and occasional very light drizzle have resulted in temperatures hovering from the 55 to 60 degree range for most locations. Temperatures are expected to show little movement through the night tonight as warm advection offsets most of diurnal tendencies. Overnight, the aforementioned lead short wave should shift east of the area allowing for mid/upper level height rises to build back in from the west.

The attention for Tuesday will be on evolution of large scale upper trough, and in particular the upper height minimum across the southern Plains that will lift north as an upper jet streak progresses through the downstream portion of this trough. Moisture transport will steadily increase in advance of this trough tonight into Tuesday, but with better mid/upper dynamics displaced well west/southwest, will maintain a dry forecast through the day. Windy and warm conditions will be the main story on Tuesday as near term buffer soundings suggest some potential of tapping into 30 to 35 knot top of boundary layer flow across northwest/portions of north central Indiana during the afternoon hours. Thickening mid cloud and moistening low/mid levels could result in mixing heights not being maximized however. Based on magnitude of low level thermals alone, highs should approach record levels in the middle 70s for Tuesday.

Categorical PoPs have been maintained for Tuesday night into Wednesday AM due to the combination of initial LLJ forcing followed by passage of cold front. While cannot completely rule out an isolated storm with some very weak elevated instability Tuesday night, any instability of note will likely be confined well south across the Lower MS Valley. With stronger upper dynamics expected to bypass the area to the southwest/south, frontal convergence fields are a bit weaker across the local area with a camp of guidance still suggesting more limited rainfall amounts. Given progressive nature of LLJ axis and low level baroclinic zone, this does not look to be a significant hydro event.

Dry slot punches into the area from southwest to northeast on Wednesday, withs some additional rain showers possible Thursday night through Friday as better moisture profiles wrap around the slowly eastward meandering upper low. Some lake enhancement is expected with the rain showers, but instability depths are quite limited without any push of dramatically colder mid level air.

The large scale pattern will favor additional influence from an eastern Pacific jet streak in closing off another system across central CONUS for the weekend but confidence in timing these additional rain chances is low. Given expected weak baroclinicity in this pattern and preference to systems more vertically stacked in nature, no significant rainfall events are anticipated through the next 7 days. Temperatures should trend near normal by this weekend into early next week.
