.DISCUSSION... Issued at 140 AM EDT Thu Aug 13 2026

A welcome (even if brief) respite from rain this morning. Main concern in the immediate near term is potential for some fog again this morning given light winds and ample near-surface moisture. Complicating factor this morning is large area of stratus that developed earlier. This stratus is dissipating but guidance struggled to capture this and subsequently the modeled widespread, dense fog is very much in question. Will continue to monitor closely through the morning but not seeing enough signals to warrant a dense fog advisory just yet.

Unfortunately, chances for rain return quickly later today and especially tonight as we remain in the "ring of fire." The first feature of interest is an MCV associated with convection presently over NE/SD. The feature will track into our CWA during the late afternoon with our next chance of rain. However, we continue to see a step downward in midlevel lapse rates, low level theta-e advection, and subsequent instability (much like yesterday was a step down from the day before). The LLJ is much weaker this morning (evidenced by near total lack of convection in our region) and surface obs/mesoanalysis suggest yesterday`s activity nudged the front slightly further south. Even seeing some N/NE flow at 850mb into our northern CWA. All of this to say that the afternoon activity is expected to remain weak as it enters our CWA. Some gusty winds can`t be entirely ruled out though, as well as some brief heavy rain in an area that really can`t take anymore.

The far greater concern will be what happens late tonight/early Fri. Another shortwave ejects into the Central Plains and once again strengthens the downstream LLJ. Another round of strong low level theta-e advection is anticipated, though there is still some spread regarding just how strong between the NAM and other hi-res guidance vs. lower-res global models. Regardless of the details, the stage is set for another round of very heavy rain roughly 04-12Z Fri morning. The exact placement remains somewhat uncertain this far out but a concensus blend would put the highest probabilities squarely over the same area already hit hard in our far southern CWA and IND`s N/NE CWA. Our flood watch was already extended through Fri morning given this potential and high sensitivity to any rain south of US-30. An argument could be made to remove the tier of counties right along US-30 but confidence is too low to get that surgical at this point.

There is some light at the end of this tunnel though. The eastern Canada upper low keeping our region on the northeast fringe of this ridge finally releases eastward allowing the ridge (and associated storm track) to (temporarily) lift northward. Storms remain in the forecast for Sat but should be much more progressive and focused more in our northern CWA vs. south. The ridge then breaks down completely by late Sunday as a cold front crosses the area. Here again, some storms possible but weaker, more progressive and overall just a different setup that is less conducive for heavy rain. Cool and dry conditions then expected Mon through at least early Wed.
