.DISCUSSION... Issued at 512 AM EDT Tue Aug 25 2026

Surface high pressure/upper level ridge in place over much of the area today and tonight will keep weather quiet. Expect partly to mostly clear skies, light winds, and highs in the 70s, low 80s. Overnight lows will be in the 50s. Patchy fog is possible this morning.

SPC Marginal to Slight Risk for Severe Storms Wed...

The upper level ridge breaks down and shifts eastward Wednesday along with the surface high as an upper level trough enters into the upper Great Lakes. This trough will cross through our forecast area and into the Lower Great Lakes by Thursday morning. The corresponding surface low pressure crosses northern Ontario/Lake Superior Wednesday, lifting into Quebec by Thursday afternoon. This brings a cold front across our CWA Wednesday afternoon into late Wednesday night/Thu AM. There could be a few showers/non-severe storms during the late morning/early afternoon hours near Lake MI and in our MI counties before we see better forcing arrive with the front. Once that occurs, models develop a convective line of storms (possibly initially discrete) that drift roughly east-southeastward through late evening. The exact timing and point of initiation is still slightly uncertain-some start the cells over Lake MI and drift them eastward along I 80-90 before filling in further southwestward with time, others develop a broader line initially and eventually weaken on the north end, pushing the greatest threat further southwest. In some cases, it almost appears the lake breeze of Lake MI enhances the development, which would trigger storms further east of the lakeshore counties. However, most of the guidance has the best severe potential developing between 4 pm-12 am EDT. Forecast wise, have broad 40-70 percent pops following this general trend as a first start. Given the earlier onset time to coincide with peak heating, confidence is slightly higher in severe weather occurring than previous forecasts. Models are also slightly higher on mid- level lapse rates (6.5C/km)...though they still aren`t really that great. Still, much of the guidance has about 1500-2500 J/kg of mixed layer CAPE by the afternoon (some suggest higher further southwest). 0-6 km bulk shear is slightly higher than previous runs suggested as well, on the order of 25-40 kts. This is enough for supercells to develop (especially models indicating the higher end of that range). With any supercell development, we have all hazards on the table, including tornadoes. 0-1 km shear values (20 kts) and SRH values (around 100 or more), in combination with low LCL`s certainly support that. These parameters were also part of the reason for increased confidence in severe weather occurring (Medium confidence).

Mostly dry outside of a low chance (20 percent) for showers and storms along and south of US 24, mainly east of I 69 on Thursday. Northwest winds behind the front will kick up Lake MI waves to around 2-3 feet, which is a moderate swim risk. Currents are expected. Monitor the beach forecast for more details.

Surface high pressure and weak ridging builds in Friday into Sunday, allowing for mostly dry conditions. Highs will be in the upper 70s, low to mid 80s.
