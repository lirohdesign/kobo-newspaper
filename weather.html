.DISCUSSION... Issued at 157 AM EDT Wed Jul 22 2026

As high pressure builds over the Midwest and into the Great Lakes, a stretch of several days of cooler than normal temperatures and comfortable humidity starts today! This surface high pressure will allow for northerly winds to usher in cooler air; highs are only expected to be in the mid 70s with lows dropping into the low to mid 50s! This pattern change will feel refreshing and is a welcome reprieve from the heat and humidity earlier this month. Lows in the 50s, while unusual from what we have been experiencing, are not that uncommon in July. We typically average about 5 nights with lows below 60 degrees in July. The coolest night is expected tonight into Thursday morning, with widespread lows in the mid 50s. It is possible that some of our rural, low lying areas get to 50 or below. NBM probabilities currently have about a 15% chance of this tonight, but chances are less than 5% for Fort Wayne and South Bend, where lows will be a touch higher, likely closer to 52-53 degrees. Record lows are safe this week as many of them are in the mid 40s. While not record breaking, we will certainly welcome these cooler, crisp nights with lower humidity!

Although the weather is quiet, the cold front that came through yesterday has stirred up southern Lake Michigan. A Beach Hazards Statement and corresponding High Swim Risk will be in effect for La Porte County, IN and Berrien County, MI today and tonight. Northerly wind gusts to 25 kts, waves of 5 to 9 feet, and strong currents will create life-threatening conditions for swimmers and boaters. Strong rip, structural, and longshore currents are expected today. Strong longshore and structural currents will be especially present on the north side of piers. Stay out of the water and off of piers! Conditions will be much calmer on Thursday and into the weekend with low swim risks each day.

Gradually warmer into the weekend, with highs in the mid to upper 80s by Sunday. Moisture increases too, which means humidity likely goes up as the weekend progresses. A frontal system brings the next chances for rain/storms Sunday into Monday, although confidence is uncertain on exact timing. Our forecast area will be on the periphery of a large upper level ridge to our west, so if we can get sufficient instability and moisture, we may be able to get storms that ride the edge of the ridge. Severe weather is not out of the question but with high spread in model guidance, confidence is very low for Sunday/Monday at this time.
