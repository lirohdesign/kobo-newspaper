.DISCUSSION... Issued at 208 PM EDT Wed Oct 7 2026

A pleasant fall afternoon once again, through a touch breezy with southwesterly wind gusts of 20-25 mph. The pressure gradient has increased in response to low pressure over the Hudson Bay and persistent high pressure over the Carolinas. This low has a trailing cold front nosing into northern Illinois this afternoon. Abundant dry air precludes any rain chances with this front. Cooler tomorrow, especially downwind of Lake Michigan, yet southern areas will remain pleasant in the mid-70s.

Turning to Friday and the weekend, increasing opportunities for rain as the jet stream buckles in response to a Pacific Northwest trough. The downstream ridge amplifies over the eastern seaboard sending a surge of warmth poleward for the weekend. Amid this pattern change, a modest warm front may serve as the focus for isolated showers over Illinois as early as Friday. We retain a dry forecast at this time with the best focus for lift a moisture colocated to our west, yet this will be a modest feature to keep an eye on.

Of greater interest is Tropical Storm Isaias, which is forecast to be swept northward in the coming days. This weekend, the primary steering mechanism for this system will be the incoming Pacific Northwest trough and a subtropical ridge over the mid-Atlantic coast. There continues to be some disagreement in the east-west nature of the track of Isaias, and so our forecast continues to carry a 40-70% chance of rain as early as Saturday night lingering into Sunday, with the greatest rain chances well away from Lake Michigan. Significant impacts are not expected.

Another area of low pressure tracks through the Midwest later Monday and Tuesday, representing our next chance for rain, with continued fluxuations in daily high temperatures.
