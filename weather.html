.DISCUSSION... Issued at 111 AM EDT Mon Aug 10 2026

Focus of discussion and any forecast updates will be on next 24 to 36 hours with regards to severe and hydrologic concerns. Getting discussion out early to to potential storms over next couple of hours.

We are monitoring a cluster of thunderstorms from SE WI into northern IL. While some occasional severe reports came from earlier storms, the main storm of interest (HP Supercell) is rooted on the outflow boundary from the earlier storms with some hints at this moving more SE with time. Other storms continue to try to develop and expand, including far NW parts of the area. This leaves a lot of uncertainty as to what occurs overnight. Plenty of instability remains across the area, but near sfc inhibition may limit wind potential overnight. Suffice to say, risk of severe remains in play across the area. For the latest updates on the mesoscale features, monitor SPC products. In addition, a Flash Flood Watch was issued earlier to cover several counties to cover the recent rainfall and potential for some areas seeing more heavy rain overnight. This is currently in effect until 11 am EDT, but given the concerns of the convective evolution overnight no changes are planned at this time.

In the wake of the overnight/early AM storms, the atmosphere will recharge once again with plenty of CAPE and sufficient shear to bring yet another severe threat Monday afternoon and evening. Much like today, the main threat will be damaging wind along with some large hail. If any outflow boundaries linger in the area, these could help with a localized tornado threat. Also in play will be the continued threat of heavy rainfall as PWATs remain about 1.75" and warm cloud depths of 14 K to 15kft. May need to expand the flood watch in area and time depending on what evolves overnight. Sadly, trends in the models do show yet more storms developing later Monday evening into Tuesday morning (possibly the 4th round of storms since Sunday evening). Not even going to try to do anything in the forecast with this given the plethora of challenges from previous rounds of storms that cannot be fully resolved this far out.

Deepening on the location of the stationary boundary Tuesday, one more round of convection could occur focused south of US-30. Again, no changes to forecast with regards to this, just something to keep in mind with the flood threat possibly still there.

Beyond Tuesday night, models seem to push the effective boundary south of the area to give us a reprieve from the rain and storms. However, the overall upper level flow doesn`t change much so low confidence at this point and model blend will be left alone.
