.DISCUSSION... Issued at 125 AM EDT Tue Sep 29 2026

Surface high pressure is shifting east underneath mid level ridging and this keeps the area dry once again. One thing that does happen, though, is that warm advection commences bringing a return to upper cloudiness for this evening and tonight. This warm advection also helps southern areas get into the upper 70s to around 80 degree highs for Wednesday. This is especially the case because the vorticity is more focused to our north and west as opposed to overhead. The best of the instability (still less than 100 J/kg of MUCAPE) finally arrives around 00z in our northwest, but surface dew points are still below 60F and the best forcing has moved more northeastward instead of eastward into southwest Lower MI. All of this to say that it is possible that rain gets into the area Wednesday afternoon/evening, but severe weather is expected to be limited and thunderstorm chances are low, if not non-existent. This is why we have good chances to see those 80 degree highs on Wednesday is that, despite cloud cover, we have limited rain chances down south there. By the time we get to Thursday, the stalled front to our west slides eastward slowly as a trough moves east- southeastward across southern Canada. Later Thursday and Thursday night is our best thunderstorm chance of this rainy period as the NAM brings through areas of instability up to 1000 J/kg of MUCAPE.

Rain may linger into Friday morning, otherwise, the trend will be to dry out Friday afternoon as a surface high pressure system moves in. This dry period also looks to last a while as the later Saturday/Saturday night rain chance doesn`t have much model agreement at this point. So, a series of high pressure systems move through and the area remains underneath an area of subsidence as a result.

Highs in the 70s to around 80 today through Thursday, trend lower, behind the cold front Thursday night, allowing for a return to highs in the 60s and lows in the 40s for Friday through early next week.
