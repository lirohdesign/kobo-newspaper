Today you're going to the Indianapolis Zoo! Everyone knows the lions and the giraffes — here are seven animals most visitors walk right past. See how many you can find.

01. walrus

  • Where: Oceans building. The Indianapolis Zoo is one of only a few zoos in the whole United States where you can meet a walrus.
  • Cool fact: A walrus has hundreds of stiff whiskers that work like fingers, feeling around the dark ocean floor for clams. Then it slurps the clam right out of its shell like a vacuum cleaner — it can eat thousands of clams in a single meal.

02. long-tailed macaque

  • Where: Oceans building — a monkey in the ocean building? Yes, really!
  • Cool fact: That's because these monkeys love water. They are excellent swimmers and can dive underwater to grab food. Their other name is the crab-eating macaque, because in the wild they wade into the water to catch crabs.

03. gila monster

  • Where: Deserts dome (say it "HEE-la").
  • Cool fact: It's one of the very few venomous lizards on Earth — and its spit is secretly helpful! Scientists studied a special ingredient in gila monster saliva and used it to invent a medicine that helps people with diabetes.

04. grand cayman blue iguana

  • Where: Deserts dome.
  • Cool fact: This is one of the rarest lizards in the world — about twenty years ago there were fewer than 30 left in the wild. Zoos and scientists (including the Indianapolis Zoo's iguana conservation partners) helped raise babies and release them, and now there are more than a thousand. It can also live longer than most humans' grandparents — over 60 years!

05. white-handed gibbon

  • Where: Forests area. Listen before you look — you might hear it first!
  • Cool fact: Gibbons are the acrobats of the ape family. They swing hand-over-hand through the trees at up to 35 miles per hour — highway speed! Every morning, gibbon pairs sing a loud whooping duet together that can be heard a mile away.

06. red panda

  • Where: Forests area, usually up in the branches.
  • Cool fact: The red panda is not a bear and not a raccoon — it's the only animal in its whole scientific family. It has a secret "extra thumb" (really a special wrist bone) for gripping bamboo, and on cold days it wraps its big fluffy tail around itself like a blanket.

07. two-toed sloth

  • Where: Ask a keeper — sloths sleep up to 15 hours a day, so look for a furry ball in the branches.
  • Cool fact: Sloths move so slowly that tiny green algae grows in their fur — which is actually great, because it works as camouflage in the leaves. And a sloth only climbs down from its tree about once a week... to poop.
